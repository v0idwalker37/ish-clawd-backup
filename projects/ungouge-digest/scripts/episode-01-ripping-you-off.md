### ACT 2: THE BLS DATA GOLDMINE [2:30-4:45]

**[VISUAL: Zoom into a government building, then to the Bureau of Labor Statistics logo]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — the same agency that tracks unemployment and inflation — publishes incredibly detailed data on what every type of work costs across the country. 

**[VISUAL: Screen recording scrolling through actual BLS databases, highlighting construction occupation wages]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
They track hourly wages for carpenters, electricians, plumbers, roofers — broken down by metropolitan area. They track material costs. They even track overhead and profit margins for construction businesses.

**[VISUAL: Animated infographic showing the three components: Labor + Materials + Overhead/Profit = Total Cost]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
This is the same data that professional estimators use. Insurance adjusters use it. Appraisers use it. But almost no homeowners know it exists.

**[VISUAL: Split screen comparison chart]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
For example, let's say you're in Austin, Texas. According to BLS data, a skilled carpenter makes about thirty-two dollars per hour on average. An experienced roofer makes about twenty-eight. Add in payroll taxes, insurance, and overhead, and the real cost to a contractor is about 1.4 times the base wage.

**[VISUAL: Animated calculator showing the math: $32/hr × 1.4 = $44.80 effective hourly cost]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
So when a contractor charges you for carpentry work, a fair price is around forty-five to sixty dollars per hour, depending on complexity. Not one-fifty. Not two hundred.

**[VISUAL: Red X animation over inflated prices]**

### ACT 3: REAL EXAMPLE - THE ROOF REPLACEMENT [4:45-7:00]

**[VISUAL: Google Earth zoom into a suburban neighborhood, then to a 3D model of a typical 2,000 sq ft ranch home]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
Let's walk through a real example. Meet Sarah — not her real name — who got a quote to replace the roof on her 2,000-square-foot home in Columbus, Ohio.

**[VISUAL: Animated quote document appearing on screen]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
The quote: thirty-two thousand dollars. The contractor said it was a "standard price" for architectural shingles with a thirty-year warranty. No itemization. Just a total.

**[VISUAL: Document gets a red "?" stamp]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
So let's break it down using actual data.

**[VISUAL: Animated breakdown chart building piece by piece]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
First, materials. Twenty squares of architectural shingles at roughly one hundred dollars per square: two thousand dollars. Underlayment, drip edge, ridge cap, fasteners: another eight hundred. Total materials: twenty-eight hundred dollars.

**[VISUAL: Running total counter in corner: $2,800]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
Labor. A competent roofing crew of four workers can typically tear off and install a roof this size in two days. In Columbus, roofer wages average about twenty-six dollars per hour. With overhead multiplier, that's about thirty-seven dollars effective cost. Four workers, sixteen hours each, sixty-four total hours at seventy-five dollars per hour — that's forty-eight hundred dollars.

**[VISUAL: Counter updates: $2,800 + $4,800 = $7,600]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
Dump fees for the old shingles: about six hundred. Equipment rental and incidentals: four hundred. Permits: two hundred.

**[VISUAL: Counter updates: $7,600 + $1,200 = $8,800]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
So our total direct cost is eighty-eight hundred dollars. Now, contractors need to make a profit — that's fair. Industry standard is between fifteen and twenty-five percent markup. Let's say twenty percent.

**[VISUAL: Final calculation: $8,800 × 1.20 = $10,560]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
Ten thousand, five hundred sixty dollars. 

**[VISUAL: Split screen: Left shows $32,000 quote, right shows $10,560 data-based estimate, with red arrow showing the $21,440 difference]**

**[VOICEOVER]**  
Sarah was quoted thirty-two thousand. The data says it should cost around ten to twelve thousand. She was about to overpay by twenty-one thousand dollars — a two-hundred-percent markup.
