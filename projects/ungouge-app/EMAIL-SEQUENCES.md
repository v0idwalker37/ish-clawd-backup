### Email 2: Educational Content (Day 3 if no quote submitted)

**Subject:** How to spot overpricing before you even get a quote

**Body:**

Hey {{name}},

Most people wait until they have a quote to verify pricing. Smart move.

But you can spot red flags *before* you even get quoted.

**🚩 Red flags when requesting quotes:**

1. **Contractor refuses to itemize** — "I don't break down my pricing." Walk away.

2. **Pressure to commit before seeing numbers** — "Let me know if you want to move forward, then I'll send pricing." Nope.

3. **Vague about materials** — "We use quality products." Which products? Brands? Models?

4. **No mention of permits** — If your project requires permits (most structural work does), they should bring it up.

5. **Can't provide references** — Every legit contractor has happy customers. Ask for 3.

**Pro tip:** Before requesting quotes, browse our [state-specific cost guides](/locations) to get a sense of fair pricing in your area.

Then when quotes arrive, you'll already know if they're in the ballpark.

## Email Design Specs

**Plain text vs HTML:**
- Use **plain text** for all transactional/personal emails (welcome, follow-ups)
- Use **HTML** for newsletters, seasonal campaigns

**Why plain text?**
- Higher open rates (feels personal, not marketing)
- Better deliverability (less likely to hit spam)
- Matches founder-to-customer vibe

**From address:** Jason <jason@ungouge.ai> (not noreply@)

**Tone:** Conversational, helpful, founder-to-customer (not corporate)

## A/B Testing Ideas

**Subject lines:**
- Curiosity ("You won't believe what we found...") vs Direct ("Your quote is 22% overpriced")
- Personalization ("{{name}}, your report is ready") vs Generic ("Report ready")
- Urgency ("Last chance") vs Informational ("Reminder")

**CTA placement:**
- Top of email vs Bottom
- Button vs Text link
- Single CTA vs Multiple options

**Content length:**
- Short (3 sentences) vs Long (3 paragraphs)

**Sender name:**
- "Jason from UnGouge" vs "UnGouge Team" vs just "Jason"
