# 20 Questions to Ask a Contractor Before You Hire Them

*Meta Title: 20 Questions to Ask a Contractor Before Hiring (2026 Checklist)*
*Meta Description: Don't hire a contractor without asking these 20 questions first. Protect yourself, your home, and your budget with this expert checklist.*

---

The moment you're about to hand someone tens of thousands of dollars to work on your home is not the time to find out they're uninsured, unlicensed, or have a history of abandoned projects.

These 20 questions create a filter. Most legitimate contractors will answer them without hesitation. Hesitation itself is data.

---

## Questions About Licensing and Insurance

### 1. Are you licensed for this type of work in my state?

Licensing requirements vary by state and project type. A general contractor may need a state license AND city/county permits for specific work. Ask for their license number — then verify it yourself at your state's contractor licensing board website.

**What a good answer sounds like:** "Yes, here's my license number: [#]. You can verify it at [state website]."

**Red flag:** Vague answers, "licenses aren't required for this," or excuses about why they can't provide it.

### 2. Can you provide a certificate of insurance?

You need two types:
- **General liability** (covers property damage during the project)
- **Workers' compensation** (covers injuries to workers on your property)

Ask for the insurance certificate directly — don't just take their word for it. Call the insurance company to verify the policy is current.

**What you need:** A certificate showing minimum $1M general liability. Workers' comp required if they have employees.

**Red flag:** "I'm a one-person shop, I don't need that." Even solo contractors should carry general liability.

### 3. Are your subcontractors also licensed and insured?

Many contractors subcontract specialty work (electrical, plumbing, HVAC). Ask if those subs carry their own insurance or fall under the general contractor's policy.

**Why it matters:** If an unlicensed electrician does your wiring and there's a fire, your homeowner's insurance may deny the claim.

## Questions About Experience and References

### 4. How long have you been in business?

Longevity matters. A contractor who has been operating for 5+ years under the same business name has a verifiable track record. New businesses aren't automatically bad, but they warrant more scrutiny.

**Follow-up:** "Under this business name?" (Some contractors rebrand to escape bad reviews.)

### 5. Have you done projects similar to mine in the past year?

Roofing and kitchen remodels are very different skills. Make sure their recent experience matches your project type, scale, and materials.

**What to ask for:** Photos of 2-3 similar recent projects.

### 6. Can you provide references from your last 3 jobs?

"References" without contact information are useless. Ask for names and phone numbers of recent clients. Then call them.

**Questions to ask references:**
- Did they finish on time?
- Did the final cost match the estimate?
- Would you hire them again?
- Were there any issues, and how did they handle them?

### 7. Can I see any completed projects in person?

For large projects, ask to visit a completed job. Seeing finished work in person tells you things photos don't.

## Questions About the Project Itself

### 8. Who will be on-site managing the work each day?

Sometimes the contractor you're meeting with won't be the one doing the work. Know who the day-to-day supervisor will be.

**Red flag:** "It'll be a crew" with no named supervisor. Someone accountable should be on-site daily.

### 9. Will you be using subcontractors? Who are they?

Get names of specific subs for specialty work. This lets you verify their licenses independently.

### 10. How long will this project take?

Get a realistic timeline with milestone dates. Not just "4-6 weeks" — ask what each phase looks like.

**Follow-up:** "What could cause delays, and how do you handle them?"

### 11. What permits are required, and who pulls them?

The contractor should pull permits, not you. If they ask you to pull permits, that's a red flag — it transfers liability to you.

**Why it matters:** Work done without permits can cause problems when you sell your home (title search reveals unpermitted work) or if you file an insurance claim.

## Questions About the Quote and Cost

### 12. Is this a fixed-price contract or a cost-plus arrangement?

- **Fixed price:** You pay the agreed amount regardless of actual costs (contractor bears the risk)
- **Cost-plus:** You pay actual costs plus a markup percentage (you bear the risk of overruns)

Fixed price is generally better for homeowners on defined projects. Cost-plus makes sense for renovation work with unknowns.

### 13. What's included in this quote, and what's not?

Get explicit exclusions in writing. Common "extras" that aren't in the base quote:
- Haul-away and disposal fees
- Permits and inspection fees
- Material price escalation
- Work discovered after walls are opened

### 14. What's your payment schedule?

**Normal:** 10-30% deposit, progress payments tied to milestones, 10% final payment upon completion.

**Red flag:** Large upfront payments (>50%), or full payment before work starts. This is one of the most common contractor scam patterns.

### 15. How do you handle change orders?

Any work beyond the original scope should be in writing before it happens, with an agreed price. Ask how they document and price change orders.

## Questions About Their Business Practices

### 16. Do you have a physical business address?

Not just a PO box. A physical location means they have an established presence that can be found if problems arise.

**Red flag:** Only a cell phone number and no address.

### 17. Have you had any judgments or liens filed against you?

You can check this at your county courthouse or through a quick online search. Contractors with a pattern of disputes, unpaid suppliers, or liens against properties they've worked on are high-risk.

### 18. What warranty do you offer on your work?

Industry standard: 1 year on labor, manufacturer warranty on materials. Some contractors offer longer warranties on specific project types.

**Get it in writing:** A verbal warranty is worth nothing.

### 19. How do you handle disputes?

Ask hypothetically: if there's a disagreement about the quality of work or cost, what's the process? Look for reasonable answers (direct conversation first, then mediation if needed).

**Red flag:** "That never happens with my work." Every contractor has had disputes; how they're resolved is what matters.

### 20. Can I have everything in writing?

This isn't really a question — it's a requirement. Every agreement should be in a signed contract before work starts. If a contractor resists written contracts, walk away.

---

## How to Use This Checklist

**Before the meeting:** Print this list. Bring it.

**During the meeting:** Take notes. Pay attention to how they answer, not just what they say. Confidence and specificity are good signs. Vagueness, irritation, or deflection are bad signs.

**After the meeting:** Verify licenses and insurance independently. Call references. Check reviews on Google, BBB, and your state contractor board.

**The gut check:** After meeting with 2-3 contractors, you'll have a sense of who felt straight with you. Trust that. The lowest bid from someone who felt evasive is not a good deal.
