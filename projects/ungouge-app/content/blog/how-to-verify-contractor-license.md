# How to Verify a Contractor's License (Step-by-Step for Every State)

*Meta Title: How to Verify a Contractor's License in Any State (2026 Guide)*
*Meta Description: Don't take a contractor's word for it. Here's exactly how to verify contractor licenses in every state — with direct links to official lookup tools.*

---

A contractor can hand you a business card, a professional-looking estimate, and a confident handshake — and still be completely unlicensed. It happens every day.

Verifying a contractor's license takes 5 minutes. Hiring an unlicensed contractor can cost you tens of thousands in repairs, legal fees, or denied insurance claims.

Here's exactly how to do it.

---

## Step 1: Get Their License Number

Before you can verify anything, you need the number. Ask directly: "Can you give me your contractor license number?"

A licensed contractor gives you this without hesitation. They know where to find it, they're proud of it, and they understand why you're asking.

If they say:
- "I don't have it with me right now" → Ask them to text it to you today
- "I don't know the number off the top of my head" → Acceptable. Still ask them to send it.
- "We don't need a license for this type of work" → Verify this claim yourself (some work is genuinely exempt; much isn't)
- "We're licensed, don't worry about it" → This is not an answer. Do not accept it.

---

## Step 2: Identify Which License You Need to Verify

Licensing is not one-size-fits-all. A contractor may hold:

**General contractor license** — Covers overall project management; may or may not cover trade work directly.

**Trade-specific licenses:**
- Electrical contractor license (required in every state)
- Plumbing contractor license (required in every state)
- HVAC/mechanical license (required in most states)
- Roofing license (required in some states)

**What to check:**
- If it's a general contractor doing all work, verify their GC license
- If they're subcontracting electrical/plumbing/HVAC, verify those subs' trade licenses separately

## Step 4: What to Look For in the Results

When you find the license record, verify:

**✅ License is active/current** — Not expired, suspended, or revoked

**✅ Name matches** — The business name and/or owner name should match who you're dealing with

**✅ License type covers your project** — A residential remodeling license is different from a commercial license or a specialty trade license

**✅ Expiration date** — Some states show renewal dates; make sure it's current

**✅ Any complaints or disciplinary action** — Many state boards show complaint history; one complaint among hundreds of jobs is very different from a pattern of complaints

---

## Step 5: Verify Insurance Separately

A contractor license does NOT mean they're insured. These are completely separate.

**To verify insurance:**
1. Ask for a "Certificate of Insurance" — this is a standard document insurers issue
2. The certificate shows: insurer name, policy number, coverage amounts, expiration date
3. Call the insurance company directly (the number on the certificate) and confirm the policy is active and covers the contractor's work

**Minimum coverage you should require:**
- General liability: $1,000,000 per occurrence / $2,000,000 aggregate
- Workers' compensation: Required if they have employees

---

## Step 6: Check Additional Sources

After verifying the license, 5 more minutes of research:

**Better Business Bureau (bbb.org):** Look for complaint history and how disputes were resolved.

**Google Reviews:** Read the 1-star reviews specifically. Look for patterns (abandoned projects, billing disputes, poor workmanship).

**Your State's Court Records:** Many states have online civil court databases. Search the contractor's business name and owner name for lawsuits, judgments, or liens.

**PACER (Federal Courts):** Check if they've been involved in federal cases (bankruptcy is sometimes a warning sign).

---

## What If They're Not Licensed?

Some legitimate contractors work in states with no licensing requirement for their trade, or do work below the licensing threshold. This is worth understanding, not automatically disqualifying.

**How to evaluate unlicensed contractors:**
- Confirm that licensing truly isn't required (call your local building department)
- Put extra weight on references and completed work verification
- Ensure they're still properly insured
- Get a detailed written contract
- Consider holding a larger percentage of payment until project completion

**When to walk away:**
- They claim licensing isn't required but can't explain why
- They resist providing insurance documentation
- They can't provide verifiable references
- They're doing electrical, plumbing, or HVAC work (always licensed trades)

---

## The 5-Minute Verification Checklist

```
□ Asked for and received license number
□ Verified license is active at state licensing board website
□ License type covers this specific project
□ Requested and received Certificate of Insurance
□ Called insurer to confirm policy is active
□ Checked BBB for complaint history
□ Searched Google reviews (read the 1-stars)
□ Asked for 3 references with phone numbers
□ Called at least 2 references
```
