# Understanding Contractor Insurance: What Coverage You Need to See Before Hiring

**TL;DR:** Before hiring any contractor, verify they carry general liability insurance ($1-2M minimum) and workers' compensation (if they have employees). Get a Certificate of Insurance (COI) directly from their insurer. If a contractor is uninsured and someone gets hurt on your property, **you** could be liable.

---

## Why Contractor Insurance Matters (to You)

When a contractor works on your property, you're assuming risk:
- Worker falls off your roof → medical bills
- Contractor damages your plumbing → repair costs
- Subcontractor's employee gets injured → lawsuit

**Without proper insurance, these costs fall on you.**

Many homeowners assume contractors "must" be insured. They don't. Licensing requirements vary by state, and even licensed contractors can let policies lapse.

---

## The 3 Essential Types of Contractor Insurance

### 1. General Liability Insurance
**What it covers:**
- Property damage (contractor breaks your window, damages flooring, etc.)
- Bodily injury to third parties (neighbor injured by falling debris)
- Completed operations (roof leaks after project is done)

**Minimum recommended coverage:**
- $1,000,000 per occurrence
- $2,000,000 aggregate

**Why it matters:** If a contractor damages your home and isn't insured, your only recourse is to sue them personally. Most contractors don't have $50,000 sitting around to repair structural damage.

**Red flag:** "I'll charge you less if you don't make me file an insurance claim." Translation: They're underinsured or uninsured.

---

### 2. Workers' Compensation Insurance
**What it covers:**
- Medical expenses for injured workers
- Lost wages during recovery
- Disability benefits
- Death benefits for families

**Who needs it:**
- Required in most states if the contractor has employees
- Sole proprietors (one-person crews) may be exempt

**Why it matters:** If an uninsured worker gets hurt on your property, **you could be held liable** for their medical bills and lost wages. This can easily exceed $100,000 for serious injuries.

**How to verify:**
- Ask for their workers' comp certificate
- Check their state's workers' comp board website
- If they claim to be "self-employed" with no employees, get it in writing

**Red flag:** "My guys are all independent contractors, so I don't need workers' comp." This is often incorrect — the IRS and state labor boards may still classify them as employees.

---

### 3. Umbrella/Excess Liability (Optional, But Good)
**What it covers:**
- Additional liability coverage beyond general liability limits
- Protects contractors from catastrophic claims

**Typical coverage:**
- $1-5 million in excess of general liability

**Why it matters:** For major projects (roof replacement, foundation work, additions), umbrella coverage indicates a contractor takes risk seriously.

**When to require it:**
- Projects over $100,000
- Structural work (foundation, framing, roofing)
- High-risk work (tree removal near power lines, excavation)

## How to Verify Contractor Insurance (Step-by-Step)

### Step 1: Request a Certificate of Insurance (COI)
**What to ask for:**
> "Can you provide a current Certificate of Insurance showing general liability and workers' compensation coverage?"

**What you should receive:**
- ACORD 25 form (standard insurance certificate)
- Issued within the last 30-60 days
- Lists coverage types, policy numbers, limits, expiration dates

### Step 2: Verify Directly with the Insurance Company
**Why:** Certificates can be faked. Always confirm with the insurer.

**How:**
1. Find the insurance company's name and policy number on the COI
2. Call the insurer's verification line (listed on COI)
3. Ask: "Is policy #[XXX] currently active and in good standing?"

**What to listen for:**
- "Yes, the policy is active" ✅
- "That policy was canceled on [date]" 🚩
- "We have no record of that policy number" 🚩🚩🚩

### Step 3: Check Coverage Amounts
**Minimum acceptable limits:**
- General Liability: $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate
- Workers' Comp: State minimum (varies by state)

**Red flags:**
- Coverage amounts below $500K
- Policy expires before project completion
- Named insured doesn't match contractor's business name

### Step 4: Confirm You're Listed as "Additional Insured"
**What it means:** If the contractor causes damage, you can file a claim directly with their insurer (instead of suing the contractor first).

**How to request:**
> "Please add me as an additional insured on your general liability policy for the duration of this project."

**Cost:** Usually $0-50 (contractors do this routinely)

**Red flag:** Contractor refuses or says it's "too expensive." This is a standard request.

## What If a Contractor Doesn't Have Insurance?

### Scenario 1: Small Jobs ($500-2,000)
**Examples:** Interior painting, minor drywall repair, gutter cleaning

**Options:**
- Accept the risk if you trust them (and get a signed waiver)
- Verify your homeowner's insurance covers contractor-caused damage
- Require they purchase a short-term policy (possible for ~$500/year)

**Not recommended for:** Anything involving heights, electrical, plumbing, or structural work.

### Scenario 2: Major Projects ($5,000+)
**Examples:** Roof replacement, kitchen remodel, foundation repair

**Recommendation:** Walk away. Do not proceed without insurance.

**Why:** A $20,000 project gone wrong can easily cause $50,000+ in damage. If the contractor isn't insured, you're paying to fix it.

## Common Contractor Insurance Scams

### 1. Expired Policy Certificate
**How it works:** Contractor provides an old COI from when they *used* to have insurance.

**How to catch it:** Check the issue date and expiration date. Call the insurer to confirm active status.

### 2. Fake COI
**How it works:** Contractor creates a fake ACORD 25 form using a template.

**How to catch it:** Always verify directly with the insurance company. Never accept a COI without calling to confirm.

### 3. "My Buddy Insured Me"
**How it works:** Contractor claims a friend or relative added them to their policy.

**How to catch it:** Ask for the policy in writing. Legitimate insurers don't do informal "favors."

### 4. "I'm Bonded, So I Don't Need Insurance"
**How it works:** Contractor confuses bonding with insurance (or hopes you will).

**Reality:**
- **Bonding** = financial guarantee to complete the project
- **Insurance** = protection against damage and injury

**You need both.** Being bonded does **not** replace insurance.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

### If You Hired an Insured Contractor
1. **Document everything:** Photos, videos, written description of damage
2. **Contact the contractor first:** Give them a chance to fix it
3. **File a claim with their insurer:** Use the policy number from your COI
4. **Contact your homeowner's insurance if needed:** They may pursue the contractor's insurer for you

### If You Hired an Uninsured Contractor
1. **Document everything** (same as above)
2. **Send a demand letter:** Outline the damage and request compensation
3. **File in small claims court:** For damages under your state's limit ($5K-$25K)
4. **Hire an attorney:** For major damage or injury claims
5. **Contact your homeowner's insurance:** They may cover contractor-caused damage (check your policy)

**Expect:** This will take months and may not result in full recovery.

## Questions to Ask Your Contractor About Insurance

1. **"Can you provide a current Certificate of Insurance?"**
   - If they say "I'll email it to you," set a deadline (24-48 hours).

2. **"How long have you carried this policy?"**
   - Long-term coverage = more credible. "I just got it last month" = possible red flag.

3. **"What's your insurance company's A.M. Best rating?"**
   - A- or better = financially stable insurer. Below B+ = insurer may not pay claims.

4. **"Have you ever had a claim denied?"**
   - This is a softball question — just see how they respond. Defensive = concerning.

5. **"Will you add me as an additional insured?"**
   - Should be a non-issue. Refusal = walk away.

## The Bottom Line

**Never hire a contractor without verifying insurance.** It costs you nothing to check and could save you tens of thousands of dollars.

**Minimum acceptable coverage:**
- General liability: $1M/$2M
- Workers' comp (if they have employees)
- Certificate of Insurance (COI) issued within 60 days
- You listed as additional insured

**If a contractor resists providing insurance proof, that's your answer.** Move on.
