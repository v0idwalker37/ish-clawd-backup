# Contractor Payment Schedule Best Practices: Protect Yourself from Scams and Abandonment

**TL;DR:** Never pay more than 10-15% upfront. Use milestone-based payments tied to completed work. Withhold 10-20% until final inspection passes. If a contractor demands 50%+ upfront or full payment before completion, walk away — it's a common scam.

---

## Why Payment Schedules Matter

**The homeowner's nightmare scenario:**
1. Pay contractor 50% upfront to "buy materials"
2. Contractor ghosts after 2 days of work
3. You're out $15,000 with a half-finished kitchen
4. Suing them costs more than you'd recover

**This happens thousands of times per year.** According to the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center, home repair fraud cost Americans $300+ million in 2025 alone.

**A proper payment schedule protects you by:**
- Ensuring work progresses before money changes hands
- Giving contractors enough cash flow to operate (legitimate contractors need this)
- Creating leverage if quality issues arise
- Reducing financial exposure if the contractor abandons the project

## The Standard Payment Schedule (Most Projects)

### Safe Structure for Projects $5,000 - $100,000

| Milestone | % of Total | When to Pay | What Should Be Done |
|-----------|------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Deposit** | 10-15% | Upon contract signing | Nothing yet — this reserves their schedule |
| **Materials Delivery** | 25-35% | When materials arrive on-site | Materials delivered (verify before paying) |
| **Halfway Point** | 25-35% | 50% of work completed | Rough work done, inspections passed |
| **Substantial Completion** | 15-25% | 90% complete, passes walkthrough | Punchlist created, minor items remain |
| **Final Payment** | 10-20% | After final inspection + corrections | All work complete, you're satisfied |

**Total paid before completion:** 80-90%  
**Retained until final approval:** 10-20%

---

### Example: $40,000 Kitchen Remodel

| Payment | Amount | Trigger | Status |
|---------|--------|---------|--------|
| Deposit | $5,000 (12.5%) | Contract signed | Not started |
| Materials | $12,000 (30%) | Cabinets/countertops delivered | Materials on-site |
| Midpoint | $12,000 (30%) | Demo done, plumbing/electrical rough-in complete, inspections passed | 50% complete |
| Near-Complete | $7,000 (17.5%) | Cabinets installed, countertops in, flooring done, painting complete | 90% complete |
| Final | $4,000 (10%) | Punchlist items fixed, final walkthrough approved | 100% complete |

**Why this works:**
- Contractor gets enough cash flow to operate (42.5% by midpoint)
- You retain leverage throughout ($11K until final payment)
- Large final payment incentivizes contractor to finish properly

## Payment Schedule Red Flags

### 🚩 Red Flag #1: 50%+ Upfront
**Contractor says:** "I need 50% down to purchase materials."

**Reality:** Most reputable contractors have credit lines with suppliers. Requiring half the project cost upfront is a cash flow red flag (or outright scam).

**Exceptions:**
- Custom-order materials (exotic tile, custom cabinets) — but verify the order first
- Very small contractors (1-2 person crews) — still shouldn't exceed 30%

**Safer alternative:** Offer to pay suppliers directly for materials (if contractor agrees, they're legit).

---

### 🚩 Red Flag #2: Full Payment Before Completion
**Contractor says:** "Pay the balance when we finish, then we'll come back to fix the punchlist."

**Reality:** Once they have your money, urgency to return evaporates. "Next week" turns into "next month" turns into ghosting.

**Why contractors do this:** They're already behind on the next project and need your final payment to fund it.

**Never agree to this.** Final payment = final inspection passes.

---

### 🚩 Red Flag #3: Cash-Only Payments
**Contractor says:** "I'll give you a 10% discount if you pay cash."

**Translation:** They're evading taxes and won't give you a receipt.

**Risks:**
- No paper trail if something goes wrong
- Homeowner's insurance may not cover unpermitted/undocumented work
- You become complicit in tax evasion
- Contractor has zero accountability

**Safer approach:** Pay by check or credit card. Yes, you'll pay sales tax. That's the cost of legal protection.

---

### 🚩 Red Flag #4: Vague Milestone Descriptions
**Contractor says:** "Pay me $10K when the work is halfway done."

**Problem:** "Halfway" is subjective. Is demolition 50%? Framing? Finishing?

**Better milestone:** "Pay $10K when rough plumbing and electrical are complete and inspections have passed."

**Why specificity matters:** Clear milestones prevent disputes. If it's in writing, there's no ambiguity.

---

### 🚩 Red Flag #5: Changing Payment Terms Mid-Project
**Contractor says:** "I need the next payment early because my supplier raised prices."

**Reality:** Either they're lying, or they're so poorly capitalized they can't absorb normal cost fluctuations.

**Response:** "The contract specifies payment upon completion of [milestone]. Let me know when that's done, and I'll pay immediately."

**Exception:** Genuine material cost increases due to tariffs, natural disasters, etc. — renegotiate in writing with updated contract.

## How to Structure Payments for Different Project Sizes

### Small Projects ($500 - $5,000)
**Examples:** Interior painting, minor repairs, small bathroom updates

**Recommended schedule:**
- **50% upon start** (or $0 if project takes <1 day)
- **50% upon completion**

**Why it's different:** Projects this small finish quickly. Contractors aren't tying up weeks of labor, so upfront payment isn't as risky.

**Red flag:** Demanding full payment upfront (even for small jobs).

---

### Medium Projects ($5,000 - $50,000)
**Examples:** Bathroom remodel, roof replacement, deck construction

**Recommended schedule:**
- **10-15% deposit** (contract signing)
- **25-30% materials** (delivery confirmed)
- **30-35% midpoint** (50% complete + inspections)
- **15-20% near-complete** (90% done, punchlist created)
- **10-15% final** (punchlist complete + final inspection)

**Key principle:** No more than 40% paid before work starts.

---

### Large Projects ($50,000+)
**Examples:** Major additions, whole-home renovations, new construction

**Recommended schedule:**
- **5-10% deposit** (contract signing)
- **20-25% materials** (verified delivery)
- **Multiple milestone payments:** Break into 4-6 stages (foundation, framing, rough utilities, drywall, finishing, final)
- **10-15% retention** (held until 30-90 days after completion)

**Why retention matters:** Defects often don't appear immediately. A 30-90 day retention period ensures the contractor returns to fix issues.

**Example: $120,000 Addition**
1. $8,000 (7%) deposit
2. $24,000 (20%) materials
3. $24,000 (20%) foundation + framing complete
4. $24,000 (20%) rough plumbing/electrical/HVAC + inspections
5. $18,000 (15%) drywall/insulation/painting
6. $12,000 (10%) finish work (trim, fixtures, flooring)
7. $10,000 (8%) final payment (after 30-day warranty period)

## What Contractors Actually Need (So You Don't Feel Unreasonable)

### Legitimate Contractor Cash Flow Needs
1. **Materials:** Must pay suppliers within 30 days (or immediately for COD orders)
2. **Labor:** Weekly or bi-weekly payroll for employees
3. **Subcontractors:** Typically paid upon completion of their portion
4. **Equipment rental:** Paid weekly or monthly

### Why 50% Upfront Is Unreasonable (Even for Legit Contractors)
**Math check:** If a contractor has $100K in annual revenue and needs 50% upfront for every job, they're essentially admitting they have **zero working capital**. That's a business on the edge of failure.

**Well-capitalized contractors:**
- Have credit lines with suppliers ($10K-$100K+)
- Maintain 2-3 months of operating expenses in the bank
- Don't need your money to start the job

**Undercapitalized contractors:**
- Use Job A's final payment to buy materials for Job B
- Constantly juggling cash between projects
- High risk of abandonment if cash flow breaks

## How to Handle "I Need More Money Upfront" Requests

### Contractor's Request: "Can you pay 40% upfront instead of 15%?"

**Good responses:**

1. **"What's the specific expense you're covering?"**
   - If legit (e.g., $12K custom order), verify the invoice and offer to pay supplier directly.

2. **"I can increase the materials payment to 30%, but I need delivery confirmation first."**
   - This protects you from the "bought materials" scam (where they pocket your money and never order).

3. **"I'm not comfortable with that. Does that affect your ability to take the project?"**
   - If they walk over a 10-15% deposit, they were a bad risk anyway.

**Bad responses:**
- "Okay, sure." (You just gave away your leverage.)
- "My buddy paid 50% and it worked out fine." (Survivorship bias — scam victims don't brag about it.)

## Payment Methods: Ranked by Safety

### Safest ✅
1. **Credit card** (best protection — chargebacks available for up to 60 days)
2. **Check** (paper trail, can stop payment if needed)
3. **Escrow service** (third party holds funds until milestones met)

### Moderate ⚠️
4. **ACH/Bank transfer** (traceable but harder to reverse)
5. **Venmo/PayPal** (convenient but limited buyer protection for services)

### Avoid 🚫
6. **Cash** (zero paper trail, zero recourse)
7. **Cryptocurrency** (irreversible, no fraud protection)
8. **Wire transfer** (impossible to reverse, common in scams)
9. **Gift cards** (if a contractor asks for this, call the police)

|-------|-------------------|-------|

## What to Put in Your Contract (Payment Terms Section)

### Must-Have Clauses

```
PAYMENT SCHEDULE
Total Contract Price: $[AMOUNT]

Payment 1: $[X] (X%) — Due upon contract signing
Payment 2: $[X] (X%) — Due upon delivery of materials (Homeowner to verify delivery before payment)
Payment 3: $[X] (X%) — Due when [SPECIFIC MILESTONE] complete and inspections passed
Payment 4: $[X] (X%) — Due when [SPECIFIC MILESTONE] complete and inspections passed
Payment 5: $[X] (X%) — Due upon substantial completion and creation of punchlist
Final Payment: $[X] (X%) — Due within 5 business days of punchlist completion and final inspection approval

LATE PAYMENT TERMS
Homeowner is not obligated to pay until work described in each milestone is complete. If Contractor claims milestone is complete and Homeowner disputes, an independent third-party inspector (mutually agreed upon, cost split 50/50) will determine completion status.

EARLY PAYMENT DISCOUNT
None. (This prevents contractors from pressuring you to pay early.)

OVERPAYMENT REFUND
If this contract is terminated early, Contractor shall refund any payments that exceed the value of completed work within 14 days.
```

## Real-World Scam Examples (Learn from Others' Mistakes)

### Scam 1: The Vanishing Roofer
**Setup:** Contractor quotes $18,000 for roof replacement. Asks for $9,000 upfront.  
**What happened:** Shows up for 2 days, tears off old shingles, disappears.  
**Outcome:** Homeowner spent $12,000 hiring a new contractor to finish + repair damage from exposed roof.  
**Total loss:** $21,000 ($9K to scammer + $12K to fix)

**How to avoid:** Never pay more than 15% upfront. Pay for materials only after delivery.

---

### Scam 2: The Fake Materials Invoice
**Setup:** Contractor shows homeowner an invoice from a supplier for $14,000 in materials.  
**What happened:** Homeowner pays $14,000. Contractor uses photoshopped invoice. Real materials cost $7,000.  
**Outcome:** Homeowner overpaid by $7,000.

**How to avoid:** Verify invoices directly with the supplier. Or pay suppliers directly.

---

### Scam 3: The Never-Ending Punchlist
**Setup:** Homeowner pays final payment when project is "95% done" with agreement that contractor will return to finish punchlist.  
**What happened:** Contractor never returns. Homeowner hires someone else to finish ($3,000).  
**Outcome:** Overpaid by $3,000.

**How to avoid:** Withhold 10-20% until punchlist is complete. Period.

## When to Walk Away

**Abandon negotiations if a contractor:**
- Demands 50%+ upfront
- Refuses to put payment terms in writing
- Insists on cash-only payments
- Gets defensive when you ask about milestones
- Pressures you to pay early ("I can start sooner if you pay now")
- Changes payment terms after signing the contract

**Your money is leverage. Don't give it away.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### "What if the contractor offers a discount for paying upfront?"
**Answer:** This is a trap. Discounts should be for paying *on time*, not *early*. Contractors who need your money upfront are undercapitalized.

### "What if I've already paid 50% and now I'm worried?"
**Answer:**
1. Demand a detailed timeline for completion
2. Inspect materials on-site (if they claim to have purchased them)
3. Request daily progress photos
4. If no work starts within 1 week, consult a lawyer

### "The contractor says Net-30 terms (pay 30 days after invoice). Is that okay?"
**Answer:** Not for residential projects. Net-30 is standard in commercial construction but gives contractors too much leverage in home remodeling. Stick to milestone-based payments.

### "What if permits get delayed — do I still have to pay?"
**Answer:** No. Milestones should be tied to **completed work**, not time elapsed. If permits delay the project by 6 months, you don't pay until work resumes.
