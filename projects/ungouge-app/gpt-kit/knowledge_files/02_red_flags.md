# Red Flags in Contractor Quotes

*Reference document for the Ungouge Quote Checker GPT*

## Critical Red Flags 🚩 (Walk Away)

### 1. Demands Cash Payment or Large Upfront Deposit
**What it looks like:**
- "10% discount for cash"
- "50% deposit required to start"
- Refuses credit card or check

**Why it's bad:**
- No paper trail = no recourse
- Cash discounts often indicate tax evasion (unlicensed operation)
- Large upfront payments fund other jobs — if they go bankrupt, you're out
- Standard practice: 10-30% deposit, progress payments, final payment on completion

**What to do:**
- Never pay more than 33% upfront
- Always pay by check or card for documentation
- Final payment only after walk-through and satisfaction

---

### 2. No Physical Address or License Number
**What it looks like:**
- Business card with only a phone number
- Can't verify license at state contractor board
- "We're new in town" or uses PO Box only
- No company vehicle lettering

**Why it's bad:**
- Unlicensed contractors can't pull permits
- No recourse if something goes wrong
- May not have insurance (you're liable for injuries)
- Often indicates fly-by-night operation

**What to do:**
- Verify license at your state's contractor licensing board website
- Ask for Certificate of Insurance (COI)
- Check for physical business address

---

### 3. Pressure Tactics
**What it looks like:**
- "This price is only good today"
- "I have another job starting tomorrow, need an answer now"
- "Material prices are going up next week"
- "I'm giving you a special deal because..."
- Discourages getting other quotes

**Why it's bad:**
- Creates artificial urgency to prevent comparison shopping
- Legitimate contractors don't need to pressure you
- Often a sign of desperation (cash flow problems)

**What to do:**
- Always take 24-48 hours to decide
- Always get 3 quotes
- If they won't wait, they're not the right contractor

---

### 4. Significantly Below Market Price
**What it looks like:**
- Price is 30-40% below other quotes
- "We'll beat any competitor by 20%"
- Too good to be true feeling

**Why it's bad:**
- May use inferior materials and substitute later
- May be unlicensed/uninsured
- May cut corners on code compliance
- Bait-and-switch: low quote, then change orders
- Contractor may abandon job mid-project

**What to do:**
- Ask specifically what's included and excluded
- Get material specifications in writing
- Verify license and insurance
- Check references for completed projects

---

### 5. Won't Provide Written Contract
**What it looks like:**
- "We can shake on it"
- "I'll write something up after we start"
- Contract is vague or missing key details

**Why it's bad:**
- No legal protection for either party
- Scope creep and disputes inevitable
- Required by law in most states for work over $500

**What to do:**
- Demand a written contract before any work starts
- Contract should include: scope, materials, timeline, payment schedule, warranty, permit responsibilities

---

## Major Red Flags 🟡 (Investigate Further)

### 6. Vague or Missing Line Items
**What it looks like:**
- "Materials: $5,000"
- "Labor: As needed"
- Lump sum with no breakdown
- No brand names or specifications

**Why it's bad:**
- Impossible to compare quotes fairly
- Easy to substitute inferior materials
- No basis for dispute resolution
- May hide inflated costs

**What to do:**
- Request itemized breakdown
- Ask for specific material brands/models
- Compare line items across multiple quotes

---

### 7. No Mention of Permits
**What it looks like:**
- Permit not mentioned at all
- "Permits are your responsibility"
- "We don't need a permit for this"
- "Permits just slow things down and cost extra"

**Why it's bad:**
- Un-permitted work can affect home sale, insurance, resale value
- Violates building codes (safety risk)
- Contractor may not be licensed to pull permits
- Homeowner can be fined

**When permits ARE required:**
- Structural changes (walls, foundations)
- Electrical (new circuits, panel upgrades)
- Plumbing (new lines, water heater)
- HVAC (system replacement)
- Roofing (in most jurisdictions)
- Additions of any kind

**What to do:**
- Ask who pulls the permit
- Verify permit requirements with your local building department
- Get permit costs included in quote

---

### 8. Door-to-Door or Storm Chaser
**What it looks like:**
- Shows up uninvited after a storm
- "We noticed your roof looks damaged"
- "We're working in your neighborhood and have extra materials"
- Out-of-state license plates

**Why it's bad:**
- Storm chasers often do shoddy work and disappear
- May inflate insurance claims (insurance fraud)
- Often overcharge and underdeliver
- Little recourse when they're gone

**What to do:**
- Never sign on the spot
- Verify local business presence
- Get quotes from established local contractors
- Don't let them handle your insurance claim

---

### 9. Unusually Long or Vague Timeline
**What it looks like:**
- "It'll take as long as it takes"
- No estimated start or end date
- Refuses to commit to timeline

**Why it's bad:**
- Contractor may be juggling too many jobs
- No accountability for delays
- Could drag on indefinitely

**What to do:**
- Get estimated start and end dates in writing
- Include penalty clause for excessive delays
- Ask about their current workload

---

### 10. Requires Full Payment Before Completion
**What it looks like:**
- "Materials are expensive, I need 75% upfront"
- Asks for final payment before walk-through
- Pressures for payment before punch list is complete

**Why it's bad:**
- No leverage to ensure quality completion
- May abandon project with your money
- Standard practice: final 10-25% held until satisfied

**What to do:**
- Never pay final payment until work is inspected
- Hold back 10-25% for punch list items
- Put payment schedule in writing

---

## Yellow Flags ⚠️ (Worth Noting)

### 11. No References or Portfolio
- May be new (not necessarily bad, but verify)
- Ask for previous clients you can contact
- Check online reviews (Google, Yelp, BBB)

### 12. Can't Explain Costs
- Good contractors explain their pricing
- Vague answers suggest hidden costs or lack of expertise
- Ask "why does this cost X?" and expect a clear answer

### 13. Negative Reviews About Similar Issues
- One bad review isn't damning
- Pattern of same complaints = real problem
- Check for complaints about: communication, timeline, quality, billing

### 14. No Warranty Information
- Should specify labor warranty (typically 1-2 years)
- Should pass through manufacturer warranties on materials
- Get warranty terms in writing

### 15. Significantly Higher Than Others Without Justification
- 20%+ above market should be explainable
- Premium materials, specialized skills, or reputation may justify
- Ask directly: "Why are you more expensive?"

---

## Green Flags ✅ (Good Signs)

### Positive Indicators:
- Licensed, bonded, insured (verifiable)
- Clear, itemized quote with specs
- Written contract with all terms
- Reasonable payment schedule (deposit, progress, final)
- Pulls permits and schedules inspections
- Provides references you can contact
- Explains the process clearly
- Answers questions without defensiveness
- Local presence with verifiable address
- Consistent online reviews over time
- Willing to wait while you get other quotes
- Clear timeline with milestones
- Warranty terms in writing

---

## Questions to Ask Every Contractor

1. "Are you licensed and insured? Can I see proof?"
2. "Who pulls the permits?"
3. "What's the payment schedule?"
4. "What's your estimated timeline?"
5. "What's NOT included in this quote?"
6. "What happens if we find unexpected issues?"
7. "What's your warranty on labor?"
8. "Can I speak to three recent clients?"
9. "Do you do the work yourself or use subcontractors?"
10. "What specifically am I getting for this price?" (materials, brands, quantities)

---

*When in doubt, get more quotes. Trust your gut. If something feels wrong, it probably is.*
