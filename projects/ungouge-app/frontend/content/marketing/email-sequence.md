## Email 3: "How It Works" Educational Email

**Subject Line:** How contractors hide overcharges (and how we find them)

**Preview Text:** The 5 sneaky line items to watch for in every quote.

---

**Body:**

Contractors aren't all crooks. But some absolutely are.

And even the honest ones will push margins if they think you won't notice.

**Here are the 5 sneakiest ways contractors pad quotes** (and how GougeAlert catches them):

---

**1. The "Miscellaneous Materials" Mystery**

**What it looks like:**  
"Miscellaneous materials: $4,200"

**Why it's sketchy:**  
What materials? For what? This is a catch-all for profit padding.

**What GougeAlert does:**  
Flags vague line items and shows you what itemized materials should cost.

---

**2. The Material Markup Madness**

**What it looks like:**  
"Quartz countertops: $8,500"

**Why it's sketchy:**  
The material might cost the contractor $3,000. That's a 183% markup.

**What GougeAlert does:**  
Shows you actual supplier costs so you can see where they're inflating.

---

**3. The Labor Rate Mystery**

**What it looks like:**  
"Labor: $12,000" (no hours, no rate specified)

**Why it's sketchy:**  
Are they charging $50/hour or $150/hour? You can't tell.

**What GougeAlert does:**  
Compares total labor cost to typical rates for that job type in your area.

---

**4. The Contingency Cushion**

**What it looks like:**  
"Contingency: 20% ($6,000)"

**Why it's sketchy:**  
10% is standard. 20% is profit padding disguised as "just in case" money.

**What GougeAlert does:**  
Flags unusually high contingency percentages based on project type.

---

**5. The Permit Fee Inflation**

**What it looks like:**  
"Permits & fees: $2,500"

**Why it's sketchy:**  
Permit costs are public record. If they're charging $2,500 for a $400 permit...

**What GougeAlert does:**  
Checks typical permit costs for your project and location.

---

**The good news?** GougeAlert spots all of this automatically.

The GougeAlert Team

## Email 7: Content / Education (Ongoing Nurture)

**Subject Line:** What's a "fair" contractor markup anyway?

**Preview Text:** Spoiler: It depends. Here's how to know if yours is reasonable.

---

**Body:**

One of the most common questions we get:

**"My contractor marked up materials by 30%. Is that normal?"**

Short answer: **It depends.**

Here's the longer answer:

---

**Material markup is standard.** Contractors aren't Home Depot. They have overhead—trucks, insurance, storage, staff, time spent ordering and managing deliveries.

**Typical markup ranges:**
- **10-20%:** Very fair, lean operation  
- **20-35%:** Standard for most contractors  
- **35-50%:** High, but sometimes justified (specialty materials, small orders, rush jobs)  
- **50%+:** Probably padding profit unless there's a good reason

**The problem?** Most quotes don't show the markup. They just say "Materials: $12,000" without breaking down cost vs. markup.

**That's where things get sneaky.**

---

**How GougeAlert helps:**

When you upload a quote, we:
1. Look up typical supplier costs for the materials listed
2. Calculate the likely markup percentage
3. Show you if it's in the reasonable range for your project type

If it's 50%+ and there's no obvious reason (like custom or specialty materials), we flag it. You can ask your contractor: "Hey, why is this marked up so much?"

**Good contractors will explain.** Bad ones will get defensive.

Either way, you learn something useful.
