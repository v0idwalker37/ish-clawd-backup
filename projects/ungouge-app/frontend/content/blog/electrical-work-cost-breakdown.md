# Electrical Work Cost Breakdown: What Electricians Actually Charge in 2026

When one electrician quotes $800 for a job and another quotes $2,400 for the same work, how do you know who's being fair? Electrical work is licensed, code-regulated, and potentially dangerous — but that doesn't mean you should accept any price. This guide breaks down exactly what electrical work costs, why pricing varies, and the red flags that signal overcharging.

## TL;DR: Common Electrical Job Costs

| Job Type | Low End | Mid-Range | High End |
|----------|---------|-----------|----------|
| **Panel upgrade (100A → 200A)** | $1,800 | $2,800 | $4,500 |
| **Outlet installation (new)** | $120 | $180 | $300 |
| **GFCI outlet (kitchen/bath)** | $150 | $220 | $350 |
| **Light fixture install** | $85 | $150 | $280 |
| **Ceiling fan install** | $150 | $250 | $400 |
| **EV charger (240V, Level 2)** | $800 | $1,400 | $2,800 |
| **Whole-house rewire (2,000 sq ft)** | $8,500 | $14,000 | $22,000 |
| **Recessed lighting (6 lights)** | $900 | $1,500 | $2,400 |
| **Generator hookup (transfer switch)** | $1,200 | $1,800 | $3,000 |
| **Smart home wiring (basic)** | $600 | $1,200 | $2,200 |

**These ranges include:** Licensed electrician labor, materials, permit (where required), code-compliant installation.

**Not included:** Drywall repair, painting, significant structural modifications.

## How Electricians Price Work

### Method 1: Hourly Rate ($75-$150/hour)
- **When used:** Small jobs, troubleshooting, service calls
- **Typical rate:** $75-$100/hour (residential), $100-$150/hour (commercial or emergency)
- **Minimum charge:** 1-2 hours even for 15-minute jobs (covers travel, overhead)

**Fair hourly breakdown:**
- Master electrician (10+ years, full licensing): $100-$150/hour
- Journeyman electrician (licensed, 4-10 years): $75-$100/hour
- Apprentice (supervised, learning): $40-$60/hour

**Trip charge:** $50-$100 separate from hourly (covers vehicle, insurance, travel time)

### Method 2: Flat Rate / Job Quote
- **When used:** Defined scope projects (panel upgrade, rewiring, fixture installs)
- **Pricing:** Based on estimated hours + materials + markup (20-35% profit)
- **Benefit:** Know total cost upfront, no surprises

**Example calculation (panel upgrade):**
- Materials: $800 (200A panel, breakers, wire, hardware)
- Labor: 8 hours × $90/hour = $720
- Permit: $150
- Overhead/profit: 25% = $418
- **Total: $2,088** (would quote $2,000-$2,200)

### Method 3: Time & Materials (T&M)
- **When used:** Unknown scope (old house rewiring with surprises)
- **Pricing:** Hourly rate + materials at cost + markup (10-20% on parts)
- **Risk:** Final cost can exceed estimate if complications arise
- **Protection:** Should have "not to exceed" cap in contract

## Detailed Cost Breakdowns by Job Type

### 1. Electrical Panel Upgrade

**When needed:**
- Adding major appliances (EV charger, hot tub, heat pump)
- Old 100A service insufficient for modern load
- Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels (fire hazard, should be replaced)
- Planning home addition

#### 100A → 200A Service Upgrade
**What's involved:**
- New 200A panel installation
- Service cable upgrade (from utility pole to house)
- Meter socket upgrade (utility company coordination)
- Re-terminating all existing circuits
- Inspection & permit

**Cost breakdown:**
- Panel & breakers: $400-$800 (depends on breaker count, brand)
- Service cable (aluminum 4/0 AWG): $150-$350 (depends on length)
- Labor: 6-10 hours ($540-$1,200 at $90/hour average)
- Permit & inspection: $150-$300
- **Total: $1,800-$4,500**

**Price variables:**
- Distance from meter to panel (more wire = higher cost)
- Underground vs overhead service (underground costs 30-50% more)
- Utility company requirements (some areas require service upgrade at the pole, adds $500-$2,000)
- Main disconnect location (NEC 2020 requires disconnect within sight of meter in new installs)

#### Subpanel Addition (for garage, addition, workshop)
**Cost:** $600-$1,500

**Includes:**
- 60A-100A subpanel
- Feeder cable from main panel
- Grounding
- 4-8 branch circuits in subpanel

**Why add a subpanel vs upgrading main:**
- Cheaper ($600-$1,500 vs $1,800-$4,500)
- No utility company coordination
- Faster (same-day vs 2-3 days for service upgrade)
- Isolates new circuits from main house (easier troubleshooting)

### 2. Outlet & Switch Installation

#### Standard 120V Outlet (new installation)
**Cost:** $120-$300 per outlet

**Includes:**
- 15A or 20A receptacle
- Romex cable run (12/2 or 14/2)
- Connection to circuit breaker
- Cover plate

**Cost drivers:**
- Distance to panel (longer runs cost more)
- Access (open walls vs fishing through finished drywall)
- Wire type (copper vs aluminum, gauge)

**Typical breakdown:**
- Material: $15-$30 (outlet, wire, box, plate)
- Labor: 1-2 hours ($75-$180)
- **Total: $120-$300**

**Cheaper options:**
- Piggybacking off existing outlet (tapping nearby circuit): $80-$150
- Surface-mount conduit (no drywall cutting): $100-$180

#### GFCI Outlet (kitchen, bathroom, outdoor, garage)
**Cost:** $150-$350 per outlet

**Why more expensive:**
- GFCI outlet itself: $25-$45 (vs $2-$8 for standard)
- Code requirement in wet locations (can't use standard outlet)
- Testing required after installation

**When required by code:**
- All kitchen countertop outlets
- All bathroom outlets
- Outdoor outlets
- Garage & basement outlets
- Within 6 feet of sink

**Pro tip:** One GFCI outlet can protect multiple downstream outlets. A good electrician will install one GFCI at circuit start ($150) instead of quoting 5× $150 for every outlet.

#### 240V Outlet (dryer, EV, welder, air compressor)
**Cost:** $300-$800

**Includes:**
- 30A or 50A receptacle (NEMA 14-30 or 14-50)
- 10/3 or 6/3 wire run to panel
- Dedicated 2-pole breaker
- Proper grounding

**Cost variables:**
- Amperage (30A dryer outlet cheaper than 50A EV charger)
- Distance (50 feet of 6/3 copper = $200+ in wire alone)
- Panel capacity (if panel full, need subpanel first)

### 3. Lighting Installation

#### Basic Light Fixture Replacement
**Cost:** $85-$280

**Includes:**
- Remove old fixture
- Install new fixture (homeowner-supplied or electrician-provided)
- Connect wiring (black to black, white to white, ground to ground)
- Test operation

**Cost breakdown:**
- Material: $0-$50 (if homeowner provides fixture) or $50-$150 (electrician supplies)
- Labor: 30 min - 1.5 hours ($45-$135)
- **Total: $85-$280**

**More expensive scenarios:**
- Chandelier (heavy, requires support box): $180-$400
- High ceiling (requires scaffolding/lift): +$100-$300
- New wiring needed (wasn't there before): $200-$500

#### Ceiling Fan Installation
**Cost:** $150-$400

**Why more than light fixture:**
- Heavier (requires ceiling fan-rated box): $30-$60 for box + install
- More complex wiring (light + fan on separate switches)
- Balancing required (wobble = improper install)

**Cost breakdown:**
- Fan-rated box: $30-$60
- Labor: 1.5-3 hours ($110-$270)
- Wiring upgrade (if single switch becoming double): $50-$120
- **Total: $150-$400**

#### Recessed Lighting (6-pack)
**Cost:** $900-$2,400

**What's involved per light:**
- Cut ceiling holes (4"-6" diameter)
- Run Romex between fixtures & switch
- Install IC-rated housings (required if insulation contact)
- Connect LED retrofit kits or bulbs
- Install trim rings
- Drywall patching & paint touch-up (often extra)

**Cost breakdown:**
- Materials (6 lights): $180-$420 (LED retrofit kits $30-$70 each)
- Labor: 6-12 hours ($540-$1,080)
- Permit (if required): $0-$150
- **Total: $900-$2,400**

**Cost drivers:**
- New construction (open ceiling) vs retrofit (finished ceiling): Retrofit adds 30-50% labor
- Insulated ceiling (requires IC-rated housings): +$15-$25 per light
- Smart/dimmable LEDs: +$20-$40 per light

### 4. EV Charger Installation

#### Level 1 Charging (120V, standard outlet)
**Cost:** $0-$200

Most EVs come with Level 1 charger that plugs into standard outlet. If you need an outlet installed in garage:
- **Cost:** $120-$200 (standard outlet install)
- **Charge rate:** 3-5 miles of range per hour (slow, fine for overnight)

#### Level 2 Charging (240V, dedicated circuit)
**Cost:** $800-$2,800

**What's involved:**
- 240V/50A circuit from panel to garage
- NEMA 14-50 outlet OR hardwired charger mount
- Dedicated 2-pole 50A breaker
- 6/3 copper wire (or aluminum if long run)
- Permit & inspection

**Cost breakdown:**
- Charger (if electrician supplies): $400-$800 (Tesla Wall Connector, ChargePoint, etc.)
- Wire: $100-$600 (depends on distance from panel to garage)
- Labor: 3-8 hours ($270-$720)
- Breaker & materials: $80-$150
- Permit: $100-$200
- **Total: $800-$2,800**

**Cost drivers:**
- Distance from panel to garage (every 10 feet adds $30-$80 in wire + labor)
- Panel capacity (if full, need upgrade or subpanel first: +$600-$2,000)
- Wall penetrations (running wire through finished basement: +$200-$500)
- Trenching (if garage detached, underground = +$500-$2,000)

**Charge rate:** 25-30 miles of range per hour (full charge overnight for most EVs)

### 5. Whole-House Rewiring

**When needed:**
- Knob & tube wiring (pre-1950s)
- Aluminum wiring (1960s-1970s, fire hazard)
- Insufficient outlets (house built before 1960)
- Buying old house that failed inspection

#### Full Rewire (2,000 sq ft house)
**Cost:** $8,500-$22,000

**What's involved:**
- Replace ALL branch circuits (outlets, lights, switches)
- Upgrade panel if needed
- Install GFCI/AFCI protection per code
- Permit & inspections (multiple stages)
- Drywall repair & painting (sometimes separate quote)

**Cost breakdown:**
- Materials (wire, outlets, switches, boxes): $2,500-$4,500
- Labor: 80-200 hours ($7,200-$18,000 at $90/hour)
- Permit & inspections: $300-$800
- **Total: $8,500-$22,000**

**Cost drivers:**
- Open access (unfinished basement/attic): Low end
- All finished walls (fishing wire, drywall repair): High end
- Number of outlets/switches (code requires outlet every 12 feet)
- Stories (two-story harder than ranch)
- Plaster walls (harder to fish than drywall): +20-30%

#### Partial Rewire (kitchen, bathrooms, specific rooms)
**Cost:** $2,000-$6,000 per room

More cost-effective if only specific areas have issues. Kitchen rewire typically highest cost (20A circuits, GFCI requirements, multiple appliances).

### 6. Specialty Electrical Work

#### Generator Transfer Switch
**Cost:** $1,200-$3,000

**What's included:**
- Manual or automatic transfer switch
- Integration with main panel
- Inlet for generator connection (outdoor)
- Wiring & breaker protection
- Inspection

**Manual vs Automatic:**
- Manual transfer switch: $1,200-$1,800 (you flip switch when power goes out)
- Automatic transfer switch: $2,200-$3,000 (detects outage, switches automatically)

**Not included:** Generator itself ($500-$5,000+)

#### Smart Home Wiring
**Cost:** $600-$2,200 for basic setup

**Common smart home electrical:**
- Smart switches (replace dumb switches): $80-$150 per switch installed
- Smart dimmer switches: $100-$180 per switch
- Neutral wire addition (many old homes lack neutral at switch box, required for smart switches): $150-$350 per location
- Low-voltage wiring (Ethernet, camera power): $120-$250 per run

**Most expensive:** Whole-home automation system with centralized control (Control4, Crestron): $5,000-$25,000+

#### Surge Protection
**Cost:** $250-$800

**Options:**
- Whole-house surge protector (installed at panel): $250-$500
  - Protects everything, best ROI
  - Required in some jurisdictions (NEC 2020)
- Point-of-use surge protectors (individual outlets): $150-$300 each
  - For sensitive equipment (home theater, computers)

## Material vs Labor Split

Understanding where your money goes:

| Job Type | Material % | Labor % |
|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Panel upgrade** | 30-40% | 60-70% |
| **Outlet install** | 10-20% | 80-90% |
| **GFCI outlet** | 15-25% | 75-85% |
| **Ceiling fan** | 40-50% | 50-60% |
| **Recessed lights** | 20-30% | 70-80% |
| **EV charger** | 35-50% | 50-65% |
| **Whole rewire** | 25-35% | 65-75% |

**Key insight:** Most electrical work is labor-intensive. Material costs are relatively small except for big jobs (panel upgrades, rewires, EV chargers with expensive equipment).

**Contractor margin:** Fair profit is 20-30% after materials, labor, and overhead. Quotes with 50-70% margin = overpriced.

## Red Flags in Electrical Quotes

### Pricing Red Flags

1. **No breakdown (lump sum only)**
   - "Panel upgrade: $4,800" with no detail = impossible to verify fairness
   - Should see: materials, labor hours, permit fees

2. **Wildly low pricing**
   - Panel upgrade for $800 (should be $1,800-$4,500)
   - Suggests unlicensed electrician, cutting corners, or bait-and-switch

3. **Excessive "trip charge"**
   - $200-$300 just to show up = padding
   - Fair trip charge: $50-$100, waived if you hire them

4. **Hourly rate over $150 for residential**
   - Emergency/after-hours: $150-$200 is normal
   - Standard daytime residential: $75-$120 is fair
   - $200+ daytime rate = overcharging

5. **Material markups over 30%**
   - Electricians mark up materials 10-30% (covers sourcing, warranty, returns)
   - Charging $200 for a $60 outlet = 230% markup = gouging

### License & Safety Red Flags

1. **"No permit needed"**
   - Most electrical work REQUIRES permit (panel upgrades, new circuits, rewiring)
   - Skipping permit = code violations, insurance issues, resale problems

2. **Unlicensed electrician**
   - "I've been doing this 20 years, don't need a license"
   - Insurance won't cover unpermitted work by unlicensed contractor
   - If something goes wrong (fire, injury), YOU'RE liable

3. **No insurance certificate**
   - Should carry general liability ($1M+) & workers comp
   - If electrician gets hurt on your property without insurance, you're sued

4. **Pressure for cash payment**
   - "10% discount if you pay cash"
   - Suggests tax evasion, no paper trail, can't pursue claims later

### Scope & Process Red Flags

1. **Starts work before permit approval**
   - Inspector can make them rip it all out
   - You pay twice (first install + redo)

2. **No written estimate**
   - Verbal quote = "I don't remember saying that" disputes later

3. **Vague scope**
   - "We'll handle everything" without listing specifics
   - Opens door for change orders and surprise costs

4. **Large upfront payment (>25%)**
   - Standard: 10-25% deposit, progress payments, 10-20% on final inspection
   - 50%+ upfront = cash flow problems or flight risk

## How to Verify Your Quote is Fair

### Step 1: Confirm Licensing
- Ask for license number
- Verify at your state's contractor licensing board website
- Check for complaints or disciplinary actions

### Step 2: Get Insurance Certificate
- Request COI (Certificate of Insurance)
- Verify it lists:
  - General liability ($1M minimum)
  - Workers compensation (if they have employees)
  - Current coverage (not expired)

### Step 3: Break Down the Quote
Ask electrician to itemize:
- Material costs (with part numbers if possible)
- Labor hours
- Hourly rate
- Permit fees
- Any subcontractor costs

### Step 4: Research Material Costs
- Google the parts (panels, outlets, wire)
- Wholesale price should be 40-70% of what's quoted for materials
- 30% markup is normal; 100%+ markup is excessive

### Step 5: Verify Labor Hours Make Sense
Use this guide's estimates:
- Panel upgrade: 6-10 hours
- Outlet install: 1-2 hours
- Rewire: 80-200 hours

If quote implies 20 hours for a 3-outlet install = padding.

### Step 6: Compare Multiple Quotes
Get 3-5 quotes. Middle cluster = market rate.

**Warning signs:**
- One quote 2x higher than others (overpriced)
- One quote 50% lower than others (cutting corners or unlicensed)

## Questions to Ask Before Hiring

- [ ] "Are you licensed? Can I see your license number?"
- [ ] "Do you carry insurance? Can I get a certificate?"
- [ ] "Will you pull permits? How much are permit fees?"
- [ ] "What's your hourly rate? Minimum charge?"
- [ ] "Can you provide an itemized quote (materials + labor breakdown)?"
- [ ] "What brand of panel/outlets/materials will you use?"
- [ ] "How long will this job take (days/hours)?"
- [ ] "Do you warranty your work? For how long?"
- [ ] "Can I see photos of similar jobs you've done?"
- [ ] "What's your payment schedule?" (Should NOT be 50%+ upfront)
- [ ] "What happens if you find additional issues (like damaged wire)?" (T&M pricing? Approval needed?)

## When to Walk Away

- Refuses to provide license/insurance
- Insists on cash payment only
- Starts work before you've signed contract
- Won't pull required permits
- Wildly low bid compared to others (50%+ lower)
- Pressure tactics ("deal expires today")
- Can't provide references or past work examples
- Vague answers about timeline or scope
- Bad online reviews mentioning incomplete work or code violations

**Electrical work isn't the place to gamble with the cheapest bid.** Fire, electrocution, code violations, and insurance claims cost far more than hiring a proper electrician upfront.
