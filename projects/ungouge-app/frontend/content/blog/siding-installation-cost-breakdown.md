# Siding Installation Cost Breakdown: What You Should Actually Pay in 2026

Siding protects your home from the elements while defining its curb appeal. When contractors quote $15,000-$45,000 for the same 2,000 sq ft house, how do you know what's fair? This guide breaks down exactly what siding installation costs — material by material, labor included, with the red flags that signal overpricing.

## TL;DR: What Siding Actually Costs

**For a typical 2,000 sq ft house (2,200 sq ft of siding including gables/dormers):**

| Material | Low End | Mid-Range | High End | Per Sq Ft |
|----------|---------|-----------|----------|-----------|
| **Vinyl** | $10,500 | $14,300 | $19,800 | $4.75-$9.00 |
| **Fiber Cement** | $17,600 | $22,000 | $30,800 | $8.00-$14.00 |
| **Wood (Cedar)** | $19,800 | $26,400 | $37,400 | $9.00-$17.00 |
| **Engineered Wood** | $13,200 | $17,600 | $24,200 | $6.00-$11.00 |
| **Metal (Steel)** | $15,400 | $22,000 | $33,000 | $7.00-$15.00 |

**Price includes:** Material, labor, trim, housewrap, removal of old siding, disposal, standard scaffolding.

**Not included:** Sheathing replacement, window trim replacement, significant rot repair, high-complexity architectural details.

## Detailed Material Breakdown

### 1. Vinyl Siding
**Why It's Popular:** Low maintenance, affordable, 20-30 year lifespan, wide color selection.

#### Cost Components (per sq ft installed)
- **Material Cost:** $0.75-$2.50/sq ft (quality varies dramatically)
- **Labor Cost:** $2.50-$4.50/sq ft
- **Trim & Accessories:** $1.00-$2.00/sq ft
- **Total:** $4.75-$9.00/sq ft installed

**Quality Tiers:**
- **Builder grade (0.040"-0.042" thick):** $0.75-$1.25/sq ft material
  - Warps easily, poor fade resistance, 15-20 year lifespan
- **Standard grade (0.044"-0.046" thick):** $1.25-$1.75/sq ft material  
  - Good durability, decent warranty, 20-25 year lifespan
- **Premium grade (0.048"-0.052" thick):** $1.75-$2.50/sq ft material
  - Best fade resistance, lifetime warranties, 25-30+ year lifespan

**Example 2,000 sq ft house:**
- Builder grade: $10,500-$12,800
- Standard grade: $12,800-$15,400
- Premium grade: $15,400-$19,800

#### Red Flags
- Quotes using builder-grade vinyl at premium prices ($8+/sq ft)
- "Premium" claims without thickness spec (should be 0.048" minimum)
- No mention of insulated backing (adds R-2 to R-3, costs $0.50-$1.00/sq ft more)
- Extremely cheap quotes (under $4/sq ft) — corners will be cut

---

### 2. Fiber Cement Siding (James Hardie, Allura, etc.)
**Why Choose It:** Fire-resistant, won't rot, paintable, 30-50 year lifespan, excellent ROI.

#### Cost Components (per sq ft installed)
- **Material Cost:** $2.50-$5.00/sq ft
- **Labor Cost:** $4.00-$7.00/sq ft (slower install than vinyl)
- **Trim & Accessories:** $1.50-$2.00/sq ft
- **Total:** $8.00-$14.00/sq ft installed

**Installation Reality:**
Fiber cement is HEAVY (2.3 lbs/sq ft vs 1.1 for vinyl) and requires:
- Two-person crews (safety requirement for large planks)
- Carbide saw blades (material dulls blades quickly)
- Pre-painting or factory color (adds $1.50-$2.50/sq ft for premium finishes)
- More precise cutting (no margin for error like vinyl)

**This is why labor costs 50-75% more than vinyl.**

**Example 2,000 sq ft house:**
- Basic James Hardie: $17,600-$22,000
- Premium with factory color: $24,200-$30,800

#### Red Flags
- Quotes at vinyl pricing ($5-$7/sq ft) — installer doesn't understand material
- No mention of ColorPlus or pre-finishing (field-painted won't last)
- Suspiciously fast timelines (proper install takes 2-3x longer than vinyl)
- Mixing brands mid-job (warranty issues, color mismatch)

---

### 3. Wood Siding (Cedar, Redwood, Pine)
**Why Choose It:** Natural beauty, repair-friendly, traditional look, properly maintained lasts 40+ years.

#### Cost Components (per sq ft installed)
- **Material Cost:** $3.50-$8.00/sq ft (species dependent)
- **Labor Cost:** $4.00-$7.00/sq ft
- **Finishing:** $1.50-$2.00/sq ft (stain or paint)
- **Total:** $9.00-$17.00/sq ft installed

**Species Comparison:**
- **Pine:** $3.50-$5.00/sq ft material — needs frequent repainting (3-5 years)
- **Cedar:** $5.00-$7.00/sq ft material — natural rot resistance, stain lasts 5-7 years
- **Redwood:** $7.00-$8.00/sq ft material — best durability, premium appearance

**Styles:**
- **Clapboard (horizontal):** $9.00-$13.00/sq ft installed
- **Board & Batten (vertical):** $11.00-$15.00/sq ft installed
- **Shingles/Shakes:** $12.00-$17.00/sq ft installed (most labor-intensive)

**Example 2,000 sq ft house:**
- Pine clapboard: $19,800-$24,200
- Cedar clapboard: $22,000-$28,600
- Cedar shingles: $26,400-$37,400

#### Red Flags
- No discussion of moisture barriers (critical for wood)
- "Maintenance-free wood" claims (wood ALWAYS needs maintenance)
- Quotes without specifying wood grade (clear vs knotty = huge cost difference)
- No finishing included (raw wood exposed = rot within 2 years)

---

### 4. Engineered Wood (LP SmartSide, Everlast)
**Why Choose It:** Wood look without full wood price, treated for rot/termite resistance, 30-year warranty.

#### Cost Components (per sq ft installed)
- **Material Cost:** $1.75-$3.50/sq ft
- **Labor Cost:** $3.00-$5.50/sq ft
- **Trim & Finishing:** $1.25-$2.00/sq ft
- **Total:** $6.00-$11.00/sq ft installed

**Better than old hardboard:** Modern engineered wood uses OSB core with resin treatment. Not the same as the Masonite that failed in the '90s.

**Example 2,000 sq ft house:**
- Standard LP SmartSide: $13,200-$17,600
- Premium with upgraded trim: $17,600-$24,200

#### Red Flags
- Confusing engineered wood with cheap hardboard
- No mention of 16" on-center fastening (warranty requirement)
- Quotes using "wood siding" without specifying real vs engineered
- Missing trim package (LP makes specific trim — mixing materials causes warranty void)

---

### 5. Metal Siding (Steel, Aluminum)
**Why Choose It:** Fire-proof, pest-proof, 40-60 year lifespan, modern aesthetic, energy-efficient.

#### Cost Components (per sq ft installed)
- **Material Cost:** $2.50-$6.00/sq ft (depends on gauge & finish)
- **Labor Cost:** $3.50-$7.00/sq ft (specialty skill required)
- **Trim & Accessories:** $1.50-$2.00/sq ft
- **Total:** $7.00-$15.00/sq ft installed

**Gauge Matters:**
- **26-gauge steel:** $2.50-$3.50/sq ft material — residential standard
- **24-gauge steel:** $3.50-$5.00/sq ft material — commercial grade, better dent resistance
- **Aluminum (.024"-.032"):** $3.00-$6.00/sq ft material — won't rust, lighter weight

**Finishes:**
- Kynar 500 coating (best fade resistance): adds $1.00-$2.00/sq ft
- Standard paint: included in base price
- Natural weathering steel (Corten): premium pricing, $10-$15/sq ft installed

**Example 2,000 sq ft house:**
- Standard 26-gauge steel: $15,400-$22,000
- Premium 24-gauge with Kynar: $24,200-$33,000

#### Red Flags
- No gauge specification (cheap contractors use thinnest material)
- "Metal siding" without steel vs aluminum distinction (very different costs)
- Missing expansion gap details (metal expands/contracts — improper install = buckling)
- Quotes combining metal siding with vinyl trim (looks terrible, kills resale value)

## Labor Breakdown: What You're Paying For

**Base labor rate:** $50-$85/hour for skilled siding installer (regional variation)

**Typical crew:** 2-3 installers

**Time requirements (2,000 sq ft house):**
- **Vinyl:** 3-5 days (24-40 crew hours)
- **Fiber cement:** 5-7 days (40-56 crew hours)
- **Wood:** 5-8 days (40-64 crew hours)
- **Engineered wood:** 4-6 days (32-48 crew hours)
- **Metal:** 4-7 days (32-56 crew hours, depends on complexity)

**What's included in labor:**
- Old siding removal & disposal
- Housewrap/weather barrier installation
- Trim installation (corners, windows, doors)
- Scaffolding setup/takedown
- Final caulking & touch-ups
- Job site cleanup

**What costs extra:**
- Sheathing replacement (rot damage): $2.00-$4.00/sq ft
- Window trim replacement: $75-$150 per window
- Soffit & fascia work: $6-$12/linear foot
- Architectural details (bay windows, gables): 25-50% time premium

## Material vs Labor Split

Understanding the breakdown helps you negotiate:

| Material | Material % | Labor % | Markup Opportunity |
|----------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Vinyl** | 30-40% | 60-70% | High (material cheap) |
| **Fiber Cement** | 35-45% | 55-65% | Moderate |
| **Wood** | 40-50% | 50-60% | Moderate |
| **Engineered Wood** | 35-45% | 55-65% | Moderate |

**Translation:** Vinyl has the most room for markup games. A contractor buying builder-grade vinyl at $0.75/sq ft but quoting "premium vinyl" at $8/sq ft installed is making 60-70% margin.

Fair contractor margin: 20-35% after materials, labor, and overhead.

## Red Flags in Siding Quotes

### Pricing Red Flags
1. **Suspiciously low ($3-$5/sq ft all-in for vinyl)**
   - Using cheapest material available
   - Skipping housewrap or proper flashing
   - Unlicensed crew (no insurance)

2. **Wildly high ($12-$15/sq ft for basic vinyl)**
   - Exploiting homeowner ignorance
   - "Premium" material that's actually standard grade
   - Padding labor hours significantly

3. **No material specification**
   - Quote just says "vinyl siding" with no brand, thickness, or warranty info
   - Gives contractor flexibility to use cheapest option

4. **Lump-sum pricing with no breakdown**
   - Can't verify material vs labor costs
   - Hard to compare quotes apples-to-apples
   - Hides where markup is happening

### Installation Red Flags
1. **No mention of housewrap/weather barrier**
   - Required by building code in most regions
   - Critical for moisture protection
   - Costs $0.30-$0.60/sq ft — skipping it saves contractor money, costs you rot damage

2. **"We'll remove old siding and see what's underneath"**
   - Professional contractors inspect BEFORE quoting
   - Open-ended quote = unlimited change orders later

3. **Extremely fast timeline**
   - Quality siding install can't be rushed
   - "We'll bang it out in 2 days" (for 2,000 sq ft) = corners being cut

4. **Cash discount**
   - Suggests operating without proper licensing/insurance
   - No paper trail if something goes wrong
   - Potential tax evasion (not your problem, but red flag for business practices)

### Contract Red Flags
1. **No warranty details**
   - Material warranty (manufacturer)
   - Installation warranty (contractor — should be minimum 2 years)
   - Missing = you're on your own if issues arise

2. **Large upfront payment (>30%)**
   - Standard: 10-30% deposit, progress payments, final 10-20% on completion
   - 50%+ upfront = contractor has cash flow problems or is a flight risk

3. **Verbal-only agreements**
   - Everything should be in writing: material specs, timeline, payment schedule, warranty
   - "Trust me" = lawyer's office in 6 months

## How to Verify Your Quote is Fair

### Step 1: Calculate Your House's Square Footage
Most houses need 10-15% MORE siding than floor space (accounts for gables, dormers, height).

**Quick estimate:**
- Single-story ranch: Floor sq ft × 1.1
- Two-story: Floor sq ft × 1.15
- Complex roofline (dormers, gables): Floor sq ft × 1.2-1.3

**Professional measurement:** Contractors should provide diagram with measurements. Ask for it.

### Step 2: Check Material Pricing
- **Vinyl:** Should see brand (Certainteed, Mastic, Alside) and thickness (0.044"+ for quality)
- **Fiber cement:** James Hardie, Allura, or equivalent with specific product line
- **Wood:** Species (cedar, redwood, pine) and grade (clear, knotty)
- **Metal:** Gauge (26 or 24) and finish (Kynar 500 vs standard paint)

Google the material cost — you should find wholesale at 40-60% of what contractor charges for material.

### Step 3: Verify Labor is Reasonable
- Take total quote
- Subtract estimated material cost (use online suppliers for reference)
- Divide by estimated crew hours (see chart above)
- Should land at $50-$85/hour for crew

**Example:**
- Quote: $18,000 for 2,200 sq ft vinyl
- Material estimate: $3,500 (vinyl + trim at $1.60/sq ft wholesale)
- Labor + profit: $14,500
- Crew hours: 32-40 hours (2,200 sq ft vinyl = 4-5 days for 2-person crew)
- Implied hourly: $362-$453/hour

**That's HIGH.** Fair would be $8,800-$10,200 for labor (32-40 hrs × 2 crew × $70/hr average) + $2,500 profit/overhead = $14,800-$16,200 total.

### Step 4: Compare Multiple Quotes
Get 3-5 quotes. Outliers (high or low) are red flags. Cluster in the middle = market rate.

**When comparing:**
- Same material quality (don't compare builder-grade vinyl quote to premium)
- Same scope (removal, disposal, trim all included)
- Same timeline (faster isn't always better)

## Common Upsells: Worth It or Rip-Off?

### ✅ Worth It
1. **Insulated vinyl backing** (+$0.50-$1.00/sq ft)
   - Adds R-2 to R-3 insulation value
   - Reduces noise, increases durability
   - ROI: Positive in cold climates

2. **Upgraded trim package** (+$500-$1,500 total)
   - Premium corners, window trim, fascia wraps
   - Better curb appeal, longer lasting
   - ROI: Recoups in resale value

3. **Proper flashing upgrades** (+$300-$800)
   - Critical around windows, doors, penetrations
   - Prevents water damage (expensive repairs later)
   - ROI: Massive (prevents $5K-$20K rot repairs)

4. **Housewrap upgrade** (Tyvek vs cheap felt, +$200-$400)
   - Better moisture management
   - Required by many building codes anyway
   - ROI: Insurance against moisture issues

### ⚠️ Questionable
1. **"Lifetime warranty" upgrades** (+$1,000-$3,000)
   - Many materials already have 30-50 year warranties
   - "Lifetime" often has fine print (pro-rated, transferability limits)
   - Verify what standard warranty covers first

2. **Foam backing thicker than 1.25"** (+$1.50-$3.00/sq ft)
   - Diminishing returns above R-3
   - Better ROI from attic insulation upgrades
   - Only worth it in extreme climates

### ❌ Skip It
1. **"Energy audit" before siding install** ($500-$800)
   - Often a sales tactic for more upsells
   - Free audits available from utility companies
   - Do independently, not through siding contractor

2. **Paint protection coatings** (vinyl/fiber cement, +$1-$2/sq ft)
   - Modern siding already has UV protection
   - Unnecessary for vinyl (color is through-thickness)
   - Fiber cement factory finish is superior to aftermarket coatings

3. **"Extended" installation warranty beyond 5 years** (+$500-$1,500)
   - Most installation defects show up in first 2-3 years
   - Paying for something unlikely to be needed
   - Better: choose reputable contractor with solid track record

## DIY vs Professional: Real Math

### Vinyl Siding DIY
**Savings:** $5,000-$8,000 on 2,000 sq ft house (eliminating labor)

**Requirements:**
- 2-3 weeks full-time work (assume learning curve)
- Tools: Circular saw, level, chalk line, tin snips, ladder/scaffolding ($500-$1,500 rental)
- Skills: Measuring, leveling, basic carpentry
- Risks: Improper flashing = water damage, unlevel install = obvious from street

**Verdict:** Feasible for handy homeowners, but time investment is REAL. Most people underestimate by 2-3x.

### Fiber Cement DIY
**Savings:** $8,000-$12,000 on 2,000 sq ft house

**Requirements:**
- Everything above PLUS:
- Heavy lifting capability (2-person job minimum)
- Carbide blades ($30-$50 each, need several)
- Precise cutting (mistakes are expensive — material is $2.50-$5/sq ft)
- Finishing knowledge (painting/staining)

**Verdict:** Significantly harder than vinyl. Most DIYers underestimate labor and mess up enough material to negate savings.

### Wood Siding DIY
**Savings:** $7,000-$11,000 on 2,000 sq ft house

**Requirements:**
- Advanced carpentry skills (especially for board & batten or shingles)
- Understanding of wood movement, moisture barriers
- Finishing expertise (stain/paint application affects longevity)
- Patience (wood is unforgiving of sloppy cuts)

**Verdict:** Experienced woodworkers only. Beautiful results possible, but failure mode is expensive (rot, warping, moisture damage).

### Metal Siding DIY
**Savings:** $6,000-$10,000 on 2,000 sq ft house

**Requirements:**
- Metal cutting/bending tools (not standard homeowner kit)
- Understanding of thermal expansion (metal moves significantly)
- Fastening precision (exposed screws, no room for error)
- Specialized trim work

**Verdict:** Hardest DIY option. Mistakes are glaringly obvious, material waste is expensive, specialty skills required.

## Final Checklist Before Hiring

- [ ] **Got 3-5 written quotes** with material specifications
- [ ] **Verified contractor license & insurance** (ask for certificate, call to confirm)
- [ ] **Checked references** (ask to see past jobs in person if possible)
- [ ] **Confirmed material brand and grade** in writing
- [ ] **Clarified what's included** (removal, disposal, trim, flashing, cleanup)
- [ ] **Reviewed warranty terms** (material + installation, length, transferability)
- [ ] **Verified payment schedule** (no more than 30% upfront)
- [ ] **Got timeline in writing** (start date, completion date, weather delay clause)
- [ ] **Confirmed permits will be pulled** (required in most jurisdictions, contractor should handle)
- [ ] **Asked about rot repair** (how is unexpected damage priced — T&M or included allowance?)

## When to Walk Away

- Pressure to sign same-day ("deal expires tonight")
- No physical business address (P.O. box or cell phone only)
- Can't provide proof of insurance
- Refuses to provide references
- Quote with no material details ("trust us, we use the best")
- Asks for 50%+ payment upfront
- Offers suspiciously low pricing with vague explanations
- Bad online reviews mentioning incomplete jobs or poor communication

**Your home's exterior isn't the place to save $2,000 by hiring the cheapest bid.**
