## Cost Breakdown: What You're Actually Paying For

### 1. **Materials (50-60% of total cost)**

#### Framing & Structure ($8-12/sqft)
- **Pressure-treated lumber for joists:** $1.50-2.50/linear foot (2x8)
- **Posts (4x4 or 6x6):** $12-35 each
- **Beams (2x10 or larger):** $15-40 per beam
- **Concrete footings:** $8-15 per footing (you'll need 8-12 for a 300 sqft deck)
- **Joist hangers, bolts, brackets:** $200-400 for typical deck

**What's fair:** Framing should account for roughly 30-35% of your material cost.

**Red flag:** If a quote says "structure included" with no itemization, ask for specifics. Under-engineered framing is a safety hazard.

#### Decking Surface ($4-25/sqft)

| Material | Cost/sqft | Lifespan | Maintenance |
|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Pressure-treated pine** | $4-7 | 15-20 yrs | High (annual sealing) |
| **Cedar** | $8-12 | 20-25 yrs | Medium (stain every 2-3 yrs) |
| **Redwood** | $10-15 | 25-30 yrs | Medium |
| **Composite (Trex, TimberTech)** | $10-18 | 25-30 yrs | Low (clean annually) |
| **PVC (Azek, Fiberon)** | $15-25 | 30+ yrs | Very low |
| **Ipe/tropical hardwood** | $18-30 | 30-50 yrs | Low-medium |

**Pro tip:** Budget composite decking often uses cheaper materials that fade faster. Mid-grade composite ($12-15/sqft) is the sweet spot for value.

#### Railings & Stairs ($1,500-5,000 total)

**Railing costs (per linear foot):**
- Pressure-treated wood balusters: $20-35/ft
- Composite with aluminum balusters: $50-80/ft
- Cable railing (stainless steel): $60-100/ft
- Glass panel railing: $150-300/ft

**Stairs:**
- 3-4 step staircase (pressure-treated): $400-700
- 5-6 step staircase (composite): $800-1,400

**Code requirement:** Most jurisdictions require railings if the deck is more than 30" above grade. Budget for it.

### 2. **Labor (30-40% of total cost)**

Deck building labor rates (2026):

| Region | Low ($/hr) | Average | High ($/hr) |
|--------|------------|---------|-------------|
| **Midwest, South** | $35 | $50 | $65 |
| **Mountain West** | $40 | $60 | $80 |
| **Northeast, Pacific NW** | $50 | $75 | $100 |
| **CA, NYC metro** | $65 | $90 | $130 |

**Typical crew:** 2-3 workers for 3-5 days (depending on size and complexity).

**What's fair:** For a 300 sqft deck, expect 60-100 labor hours total ($3,000-7,500 depending on region).

**Red flag:** If labor is quoted at less than 25% of total cost, the contractor may be:
- Planning to cut corners
- Using inexperienced crew
- Underestimating complexity (change orders coming later)

---

### 3. **Permits & Fees (5-10% of total cost)**

Don't skip permits. Seriously.

| Permit Type | Typical Cost |
|-------------|--------------|
| **Building permit** | $150-500 |
| **Engineering/structural review** | $300-800 (if required) |
| **HOA approval** | $0-200 (some HOAs charge admin fees) |

**Why it matters:**
- Un-permitted decks must often be torn down when discovered
- Affects home insurance coverage
- Becomes a liability when selling
- Can void warranties on materials

**What's fair:** Contractor should pull permits and include cost in quote. If they suggest skipping permits "to save money," walk away.

---

### 4. **Site Prep & Extras**

These are often NOT included in base quotes:

| Item | Cost |
|------|------|
| **Grading/leveling site** | $500-2,000 |
| **Removing old deck** | $800-2,500 |
| **Tree/stump removal** | $300-1,500 per tree |
| **Electrical (outlets, lighting)** | $1,000-3,500 |
| **Built-in seating/planters** | $500-2,000 |
| **Pergola or shade structure** | $2,000-8,000 |
| **Skirting (to hide under-deck)** | $3-8/linear foot |

**Pro tip:** Get these in writing BEFORE work starts. "Oh, we'll need to do X" after demo begins is how budgets blow up.

## How to Tell If Your Deck Quote Is Fair

### 1. **Check the material markup**

Contractors typically mark up materials by 10-20%. You can verify retail prices:
- Lowe's/Home Depot for common materials
- Local lumber yards for framing
- Manufacturer websites for composite decking MSRP

**Red flag:** Material costs 40%+ above retail (unless it's specialized/custom product).

### 2. **Verify labor hours match scope**

| Deck Size | Typical Labor Hours |
|-----------|---------------------|
| 100-200 sqft | 30-50 hours |
| 200-300 sqft | 50-80 hours |
| 300-400 sqft | 70-100 hours |
| 400+ sqft | 90-130+ hours |

**Add hours for:**
- Complex shapes (angles, curves, multiple levels): +20-40%
- Elevated decks (8'+ above grade): +30-50%
- Stairs, built-ins, pergolas: +15-30% each

### 3. **Compare apples to apples**

When comparing three quotes:

✅ **Do:**
- Confirm same decking material (or equivalent grade)
- Verify same railing style
- Check if permits are included
- Ask if stairs are included
- Confirm warranty coverage

❌ **Don't:**
- Compare PT wood quote to composite quote
- Assume "same" materials without brand/model names
- Ignore differences in railing quality

### 4. **Watch for lowball traps**

If one quote is 30%+ cheaper than others:

**Possible explanations:**
- Different materials (PT wood vs composite, or lower-grade composite)
- Thinner joists/wider spacing (not to code)
- Skipping permits
- Inexperienced crew
- Planning to upsell with change orders

**What to ask:**
- "What joist spacing are you using?" (16" on-center is standard, 24" is weaker)
- "What fastener type?" (hidden clips cost more but look better)
- "Are permits included?"
- "What's your warranty on workmanship?"

## What's NOT Included in Most Quotes

Unless explicitly stated, assume these are extra:

- Gas/water line relocation
- Irrigation system adjustments
- Landscaping around deck
- Outdoor kitchen/BBQ hookups
- Hot tub reinforcement (requires extra structural support)
- Snow load engineering (in heavy snow regions)

**Pro tip:** Ask for a written scope of work that lists what IS and ISN'T included.

## When to DIY vs Hire a Pro

### **DIY makes sense if:**
- You have carpentry experience
- You own/can borrow necessary tools (circular saw, drill, level, post-hole digger)
- The deck is ground-level (< 30" high, no railings required)
- You have time (20-40 hours for a 200 sqft deck)
- You're comfortable pulling permits and arranging inspections

**Savings:** 30-40% on labor (but factor in tool costs and your time).

### **Hire a pro if:**
- Deck is elevated (safety risk if framing fails)
- Local code is complex
- You need engineering (steep slope, heavy snow loads)
- You want warranty protection
- Your time is worth more than $40-60/hr

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

1. **"Can I see the itemized material list with brands/models?"**
   - If they won't provide it, walk away.

2. **"What's your payment schedule?"**
   - Standard: 10-20% deposit, 40% at demo/framing, 40% at completion.
   - Red flag: 50%+ upfront.

3. **"Are permits included?"**
   - If no, get permit cost estimate in writing.

4. **"What warranty do you offer on workmanship?"**
   - Industry standard: 1-2 years on labor.

5. **"What's your joist spacing and beam size?"**
   - Should match local code (typically 16" on-center for joists).

6. **"Do you carry general liability and workers' comp insurance?"**
   - Ask for proof. If they're uninsured and someone gets hurt, you're liable.
