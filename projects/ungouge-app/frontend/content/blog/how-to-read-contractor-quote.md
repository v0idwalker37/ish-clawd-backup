# How to Read a Contractor Quote (Without Getting Gouged)

*A line-by-line guide to understanding what you're actually paying for*

---

You've got a contractor quote in hand. It's a mess of numbers, materials you've never heard of, and a total at the bottom that makes you wince.

Is it fair? Is it high? How would you even know?

This guide walks you through reading a contractor quote like someone who's seen thousands of them.

## The Anatomy of a Good Quote

A professional quote should have these sections:

### 1. Scope of Work

This describes WHAT they're doing. Not just "roof replacement" — specifics:
- Tear off existing shingles (1 layer or 2?)
- Install ice & water shield at eaves and valleys
- Install synthetic underlayment
- Install 30-year architectural shingles
- Replace all flashing, drip edge, and ridge vents
- Cleanup and disposal

**Red flag:** Vague scope like "complete roof job" with no details. This is how change orders happen.

### 2. Materials Section

Should list:
- Product names and types (not just "shingles")
- Quantities (squares for roofing, square feet for flooring)
- Unit prices

Example:
```
Architectural shingles (GAF Timberline HDZ): 24 squares @ $120/sq = $2,880
Synthetic underlayment: 24 squares @ $15/sq = $360
Ice & water shield: 5 squares @ $35/sq = $175
```

**Red flag:** No quantities or unit prices. Just "materials: $5,000." You have no idea if they're using quality products or marking up cheap ones.

### 3. Labor Section

Should show:
- What tasks are included
- Either hours/rates OR flat labor cost by task

Example:
```
Tear-off and prep: $1,800
Installation: $3,800
Cleanup and disposal: $500
Total labor: $6,100
```

**Red flag:** No labor breakdown at all. "Labor included" hides whether they're padding this line.

### 4. Additional Items

Things like:
- Permits (if required)
- Dumpster rental
- Equipment rental
- Travel/delivery fees

These should be itemized, not buried.

### 5. Payment Terms

- Deposit amount (should be 10-33%, NOT 50%+)
- Progress payments
- Final payment terms

**Red flag:** 50% or more upfront. Reputable contractors don't need half your money before starting.

## The Math Check

Want to sanity-check a quote? Here's a rough formula:

**Materials cost × 2.5 to 3.5 = Total project cost**

This accounts for labor, overhead, and profit. So if materials legitimately cost $4,000, expect a total quote of $10,000-14,000.

If they're quoting $20,000? Ask questions.

## What's Missing = What You'll Pay Later

Common items contractors "forget" to include:

- **Permit costs** — often $200-500, legally required
- **Disposal fees** — dumpsters cost $400-600
- **Code-required upgrades** — discovered "during the job"
- **Flashing and trim work** — the details that make it watertight
- **Site prep and cleanup** — assumed, but not always included

Get clarification on all of these BEFORE signing.

## Questions to Ask

1. "Can you break out materials and labor separately?"
2. "What's your markup on materials?" (15-30% is normal)
3. "What happens if you find damage behind the [siding/shingles/drywall]?"
4. "Is this quote good for 30/60/90 days?"
5. "What's NOT included that I should know about?"

A legitimate contractor will answer these without getting defensive.

## The Bottom Line

A good quote is transparent. You should be able to trace where every dollar goes.

If a quote is vague, rushed, or pressured — that's a red flag bigger than the price itself.
