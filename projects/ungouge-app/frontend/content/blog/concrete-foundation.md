# Concrete & Foundation Cost Breakdown: Real Prices for Repairs, Pouring, and Waterproofing

Foundation work is the one category where homeowners are most vulnerable to fear-based pricing. A contractor points at a crack in your basement wall, says "structural failure," and suddenly you're staring at a $25,000 quote.

Sometimes that number is legit. Sometimes it's a $800 fix with a $24,200 markup.

Concrete and foundation work covers a huge range — from pouring a simple patio slab to underpinning a sinking foundation. The materials are relatively cheap (concrete is $100-$150/cubic yard). What you're paying for is expertise, equipment, and labor. Understanding what's actually involved helps you tell a fair quote from a fear-mongering one.

## Foundation Repair: What You're Actually Paying For

### Understanding Foundation Problems

Not all cracks are emergencies. Here's the hierarchy:

**Cosmetic cracks (non-structural):**
- Hairline cracks less than 1/8" wide
- Shrinkage cracks from concrete curing
- Surface spalling (flaking)
- **Fix:** Epoxy injection or caulk — $250-$800 per crack

**Minor structural cracks:**
- Cracks 1/8" to 1/2" wide
- Horizontal cracks in block walls
- Stair-step cracks in mortar joints
- **Fix:** Epoxy injection + carbon fiber reinforcement — $800-$3,000 per crack

**Major structural issues:**
- Cracks wider than 1/2" or growing
- Bowing/leaning walls
- Sinking or settling foundation
- Doors/windows sticking or not closing
- **Fix:** Piering, underpinning, or wall stabilization — $5,000-$35,000+

### Foundation Crack Repair Costs

| **Repair Method** | **Cost per Crack** | **Best For** |
|--------------------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Epoxy injection** | $250-$600 | Hairline to 1/4" cracks in poured concrete |
| **Polyurethane injection** | $300-$700 | Active water leaks, cracks that may shift |
| **Carbon fiber strips** | $500-$1,200 per strip | Reinforcing cracked walls, preventing movement |
| **Wall anchors/plates** | $700-$1,500 per anchor | Bowing basement walls (need 3-5 per wall) |
| **Steel I-beams** | $4,000-$8,000 per wall | Severely bowing walls |

**Typical project:** A basement with 3-4 minor cracks repaired with epoxy injection: **$1,000-$2,500 total**.

**Red flag:** A contractor recommending steel I-beams or piering for hairline cracks. Get a structural engineer's opinion ($300-$600 for inspection) before committing to $10K+ foundation work.

### Foundation Piering & Underpinning

When a foundation is actually settling or sinking, piering lifts it back to level.

| **Pier Type** | **Cost per Pier** | **Number Needed** | **Typical Total** |
|---------------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Push piers (steel)** | $1,000-$2,000 | 6-12 | $6,000-$24,000 |
| **Helical piers** | $1,200-$2,500 | 6-12 | $7,200-$30,000 |
| **Slab piers (interior)** | $1,400-$2,800 | 4-8 | $5,600-$22,400 |
| **Concrete underpinning** | $1,000-$1,500/ft | 20-40 linear ft | $20,000-$60,000 |

**What's included in piering:**
- Excavation around foundation
- Driving piers to load-bearing bedrock/soil
- Hydraulic lifting of foundation
- Backfilling and compaction
- Interior crack repair (usually)

**What's NOT included:**
- Interior finishing (drywall, paint, flooring repair after lifting)
- Landscaping restoration
- Plumbing/utility adjustments if foundation shifted significantly
- Structural engineer's report (get one independently for $300-$600)

**Pro tip:** Always hire a licensed structural engineer BEFORE signing with a foundation repair company. Foundation companies have a financial incentive to recommend maximum work. An independent engineer will tell you exactly what's needed — nothing more.

## Basement Waterproofing

### Interior Waterproofing

Interior systems manage water after it enters, channeling it to a sump pump.

| **Component** | **Cost** |
|---------------|----------|
| **Interior drain tile (French drain)** | $50-$80/linear foot |
| **Sump pump installation** | $800-$2,500 |
| **Sump pump (battery backup)** | $1,200-$3,500 |
| **Vapor barrier on walls** | $2-$5/sqft |
| **Dehumidifier (commercial grade)** | $800-$2,000 |

**Typical interior waterproofing system** (1,000 sqft basement):
- French drain around perimeter (~130 linear feet): $6,500-$10,400
- Sump pump with battery backup: $1,500-$3,000
- Vapor barrier: $2,000-$5,000
- **Total: $10,000-$18,400**

### Exterior Waterproofing

Exterior systems prevent water from reaching the foundation — the gold standard, but much more expensive.

| **Component** | **Cost** |
|---------------|----------|
| **Excavation to footing** | $50-$100/linear foot |
| **Waterproof membrane application** | $20-$40/linear foot |
| **Exterior drain tile** | $30-$50/linear foot |
| **Backfill and grading** | $15-$30/linear foot |
| **Landscaping restoration** | $1,000-$5,000 |

**Typical exterior waterproofing** (1,000 sqft basement, ~130 linear feet):
- Full excavation and waterproofing: $15,000-$30,000
- Landscaping restoration: $2,000-$5,000
- **Total: $17,000-$35,000**

### Interior vs. Exterior: When to Use Each

**Interior waterproofing makes sense when:**
- Water enters through floor/wall joints
- Budget is limited
- Landscaping/structures prevent exterior access
- Problem is moderate (occasional dampness, not flooding)

**Exterior waterproofing makes sense when:**
- Significant water pressure against walls
- Foundation coating has failed
- You're already excavating for foundation repair
- Long-term solution is priority over cost

**Red flag:** A contractor pushing a $25,000 exterior dig when interior drainage would solve the problem. Get two independent opinions.

#### What Goes Into a Concrete Driveway

**Preparation (often underestimated):**
- Demolition/removal of old driveway: $1-$3/sqft ($600-$1,800 for 600 sqft)
- Grading and compaction: $1-$2/sqft
- Gravel sub-base (4-6"): $0.50-$1.50/sqft
- Forms: $1-$2/linear foot

**The concrete itself:**
- Standard 4,000 PSI mix: $130-$160/cubic yard
- A 600 sqft driveway at 4" thick = ~7.5 cubic yards
- Concrete cost: $975-$1,200
- Fiber mesh reinforcement: $0.10-$0.25/sqft ($60-$150)
- Rebar (if specified): $0.50-$0.75/sqft ($300-$450)

**Labor:**
- Pouring crew (3-4 workers): $3-$5/sqft ($1,800-$3,000)
- Finishing: included in labor
- Saw-cut control joints: $1-$2/linear foot

**Curing and sealing:**
- Concrete sealer: $0.25-$0.75/sqft ($150-$450)

## Mudjacking & Slab Leveling

Settled concrete doesn't always need replacing. Lifting it back into place is cheaper.

| **Method** | **Cost per Sqft** | **Average Job** |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Traditional mudjacking** | $3-$6 | $500-$1,500 |
| **Polyurethane foam injection (polyjacking)** | $5-$12 | $800-$2,500 |

**Mudjacking vs. Polyjacking:**
- **Mudjacking:** Cheaper, uses mud/cement slurry, heavier, larger holes (1-2"), can re-settle
- **Polyjacking:** More expensive, uses polyurethane foam, lighter, smaller holes (5/8"), more permanent

**When leveling works:**
- Concrete is in decent condition (no major cracks or crumbling)
- Slab has settled 1-4 inches
- Subgrade compaction issue (not ongoing erosion)

**When replacement is better:**
- Concrete is broken into multiple pieces
- Settlement exceeds 4 inches
- Concrete is old/deteriorating regardless
- Subgrade has ongoing drainage problems

## DIY vs. Professional

### DIY-Friendly Projects

| **Project** | **DIY Cost** | **Pro Cost** | **DIY Savings** |
|-------------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Small patio slab (100 sqft)** | $600-$1,200 | $1,500-$2,500 | 40-60% |
| **Crack sealing (epoxy kits)** | $30-$100/crack | $250-$600/crack | 75-85% |
| **Concrete sealing** | $50-$150 | $300-$800 | 70-85% |
| **Small walkway (50 sqft)** | $300-$600 | $800-$1,500 | 50-60% |

**DIY is realistic when:**
- Project is under 100 sqft
- No structural implications
- You have time (concrete is unforgiving — once mixed, you're on the clock)
- You're comfortable with physical labor (concrete is heavy: ~150 lbs/cubic foot)

### Leave to Professionals

- **Foundation repair** — always. Structural integrity is non-negotiable.
- **Driveways and large slabs** — concrete trucks, finishing skills, and timing are critical. Bad DIY concrete shows immediately and costs more to fix than doing it right the first time.
- **Basement waterproofing** — interior drain tile requires cutting through basement floor and proper drainage design. Exterior waterproofing requires excavation equipment.
- **Any load-bearing concrete** — footings, structural walls, support columns.

**Why concrete DIY fails:** Concrete finishing is a genuine skill. Professional finishers have years of experience timing the set, floating the surface, edging joints, and applying finishes. A bad pour creates a surface that cracks prematurely, drains poorly, and looks terrible. Ripping it out costs $1-$3/sqft.

## Red Flags & Common Upsells

### Foundation Repair Red Flags

1. **"Your foundation is failing"** without data — Legitimate contractors show measurements: crack width over time, wall displacement readings, level readings. If they're just pointing at a crack and using scare words, get an independent structural engineer.

2. **No structural engineer involved** — Foundation repair companies have a conflict of interest. They profit from recommending more work. Spend $300-$600 on an independent structural engineer's assessment before committing to $10K+ repairs.

3. **"Proprietary" systems at double the price** — Many foundation companies rebrand standard products (push piers, carbon fiber strips) with proprietary names and charge a premium. Ask for the actual manufacturer and product specifications.

4. **Lifetime warranty that's actually limited** — Read the fine print. Many "lifetime warranties" are transferable only once, exclude certain conditions, or require annual inspections at $200-$500/year.

5. **Free inspection that's really a sales pitch** — Foundation companies offer free inspections because they convert at high rates. They'll find something. Always verify with an independent engineer.

### Concrete Flatwork Red Flags

1. **No sub-base preparation** — Skipping proper gravel base and compaction saves the contractor time but guarantees premature cracking and settling.

2. **3" thick instead of 4"** — Driveways should be minimum 4" (6" for heavy vehicles). Patios can be 4". A contractor pouring thin to save material cost is setting you up for failure.

3. **No control joints** — Saw-cut or tooled joints every 8-12 feet control where cracks form. No joints = random cracking.

4. **No rebar or fiber mesh** — Some contractors skip reinforcement entirely. At minimum, fiber mesh should be included. Rebar is ideal for driveways and structural slabs.

5. **Quoting by the job, not by the yard/sqft** — Vague pricing makes it impossible to compare quotes. Demand itemized breakdowns.

## Bottom Line

Concrete and foundation work ranges from simple ($500 mudjacking job) to serious ($30K+ foundation repair). The key to not getting gouged:

1. **Understand your problem first** — Is it cosmetic or structural? An independent structural engineer ($300-$600) can save you thousands by preventing unnecessary work.

2. **Get 3+ quotes** — Foundation repair especially varies wildly. We've seen the same project quoted at $6,000 and $22,000.

3. **Demand itemized breakdowns** — Per-pier pricing for foundation work, per-sqft or per-yard for flatwork. Lump sums hide padding.

4. **Verify concrete specs** — 4,000 PSI mix, proper thickness, reinforcement, sub-base prep. These aren't upgrades — they're baseline requirements.

5. **Time it right** — Fall pours are often 10-20% cheaper. Avoid emergency "your foundation is failing" decisions.

**Concrete is cheap. Labor and expertise are what you're paying for. Make sure you're paying for the right amount of both.**
