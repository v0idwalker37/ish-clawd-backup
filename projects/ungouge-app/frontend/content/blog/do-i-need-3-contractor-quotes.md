# Do I Really Need 3 Contractor Quotes?

*Published: February 19, 2026*

*Meta Description: The "get 3 quotes" advice is everywhere. But is it actually helping you, or just feeding lead generation companies? Here's what to do instead.*

Everyone tells you to get three contractor quotes. Your neighbor says it. Consumer blogs say it. Even contractors say it (because it makes them look reasonable).

But here's what nobody tells you: **getting three quotes doesn't guarantee you'll spot a bad one.**

## The Problem With "Get 3 Quotes"

Let's say you get three quotes for a kitchen remodel:

- **Contractor A:** $32,500
- **Contractor B:** $29,800  
- **Contractor C:** $35,200

Which one is fair?

You don't actually know. You just know Contractor B is cheapest. But what if all three are overcharging by 20%? Or what if Contractor B is cutting corners?

**Three quotes only tell you what three contractors want to charge. They don't tell you what the work is actually worth.**

## When 3 Quotes Actually Helps

Getting multiple quotes is useful when:

### 1. You're Comparing Scope, Not Just Price

If one contractor includes a designer and another doesn't, that explains a price difference. If one uses premium materials and another uses standard, same thing.

Multiple quotes help you understand **what's included** and make apples-to-apples comparisons.

### 2. You Already Know Fair Pricing

If you've researched typical costs for your project (or used a tool like GougeAlert), you can spot which of the three quotes is closest to fair and which are outliers.

### 3. You're Testing Professionalism

Multiple quotes let you compare:
- How detailed each contractor's breakdown is
- Who shows up on time
- Who asks good questions vs. who rushes through
- Who provides references and insurance docs without being asked

These matter as much as price.

## When 3 Quotes Wastes Your Time

### 1. All Three Are From the Same Lead Gen Site

Those "free quote" sites (HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Angi, etc.) sell your info to contractors who then mark up their quotes 15-25% to cover the lead cost.

If all three quotes come from the same platform, you're comparing three inflated prices. Not helpful.

### 2. You Don't Know What to Look For

If you can't tell the difference between a $32k quote and a $29k quote beyond "one's cheaper," you're not actually comparing — you're guessing.

**Cheaper could mean:**
- Better efficiency (good)
- Lower quality materials (bad)
- Cutting corners on permits or insurance (very bad)
- Loss-leader pricing to get in the door, then upsells later (sneaky)

Without baseline knowledge of fair pricing, three quotes just give you three data points you can't interpret.

### 3. Your Project Is Straightforward

For simple, well-defined projects (fence install, deck refinish, interior paint), pricing is fairly standardized. Getting five quotes won't reveal much beyond "this guy is expensive" or "this guy seems cheap."

One good quote + a pricing tool like GougeAlert gets you the same outcome in 1/5 the time.

## The Real Question Isn't "How Many Quotes?"

It's: **"How do I know if ANY of these quotes are fair?"**

### The Old Way:
1. Get 3 quotes
2. Average them
3. Hope that average is close to fair
4. Cross your fingers

1. Get 1-2 quotes from licensed, insured contractors
2. Upload them to GougeAlert

4. Negotiate or get more quotes if needed

You skip the guessing. You know what fair looks like before you waste time on Quote #3.

## What to Do Instead of Blindly Getting 3 Quotes

### Step 1: Get One Really Good Quote

Find a contractor with:
- ✅ License and insurance (verify yourself)
- ✅ Good references you actually call
- ✅ Detailed, itemized quote

One solid quote from a legit contractor is better than three half-assed quotes from randos.

### Step 3: Decide

## When You SHOULD Get Multiple Quotes

- **Large projects ($50k+):** More money = more due diligence
- **Custom/complex work:** Architects, structural changes, historic homes
- **First time working with this contractor:** Get one more as a sanity check
- **Your first quote came back unusually high or low:** Need a reference point

But in all these cases, **you still need a way to know what fair pricing looks like**. Otherwise you're just collecting numbers.
