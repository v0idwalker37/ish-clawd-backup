# 5 Signs You Should Walk Away From a Contractor Quote

*Published: February 19, 2026*

*Meta Description: Know when a contractor quote is unfixable. These 5 red flags mean it's time to walk away and find someone else.*

You got the quote. You read it over. Something feels... off.

Trust that feeling.

Not every bad quote can be fixed with negotiation. Some are so fundamentally flawed that your best move is to thank them for their time and move on. Here are the five signs that quote isn't worth saving.

## 1. They Won't Provide an Itemized Breakdown

**Red flag:** "Total project cost: $37,500. That's our price."

No breakdown. No line items. No explanation of what you're actually paying for.

### Why It Matters

Without an itemized breakdown, you can't:
- Compare their pricing to other quotes
- Identify where costs are concentrated
- Negotiate specific line items
- Verify you're not paying for work you don't need

**What to do:** Ask once for an itemized quote. If they refuse or push back ("we don't operate that way"), walk. Any legitimate contractor can break down their pricing.

**Exception:** Some very small jobs ($500-$1,000) might reasonably quote as lump sum. Anything over $2,000 should be itemized.

## 2. Pressure to Sign Immediately

**Red flags:**
- "This price is only good until tomorrow"
- "I have another client interested, need to know today"
- "Materials are about to go up, lock in now"

### Why It Matters

Legitimate contractors understand that hiring decisions take time. They want you to feel confident, not rushed.

High-pressure tactics are designed to bypass your critical thinking. They work because once you've said yes, it's psychologically harder to back out — even if you find problems later.

### What to do:

**Say this:** "I need a few days to review this and compare options. If that doesn't work for you, I understand."

If they won't give you 48-72 hours, they're not the right contractor. Material prices don't change overnight (despite what they claim), and good contractors have enough work that they're not desperate for your signature today.

## 3. Markup Over 50% on Materials

**Red flag:** Tile costs $4/sq ft retail, contractor quotes $8/sq ft.

### Why It Matters

Standard material markup is 10-20%. Some contractors go to 25-30% if they're providing design services or custom sourcing.

Anything over 40% suggests they're either:
- Padding the quote assuming you won't check
- Buying from overpriced suppliers (incompetence)
- Charging you retail and pocketing their contractor discount (unethical)

### What to Do

**Check 3-5 line items** against Home Depot, Lowe's, or specialty suppliers in your area. If most are 40%+ over retail, walk.

## 4. Vague Timeline or No Timeline At All

**Red flags:**
- "Should take about 2-3 weeks, maybe longer"
- "We'll start as soon as we can"
- No start date or completion date in the quote

### Why It Matters

Professional contractors schedule projects. They know when they can start, how long each phase takes, and when they'll finish. Vague timelines mean:
- They're juggling too many projects
- They don't actually know how long the work takes (inexperience)
- They're leaving themselves wiggle room to delay indefinitely

### What to Do

**Ask:** "What's the start date and estimated completion date?"

If they can't commit to a start date within 2 weeks of signing (for projects under $20k) or 4 weeks (for larger projects), they're overbooked. If they can't estimate completion within a reasonable window, they're not organized enough to manage your project well.

## 5. No Proof of Insurance or License

**Red flag:** "We're working on getting that" or "We're covered under someone else's policy."

### Why It Matters

This is non-negotiable. If something goes wrong:
- **No license:** You have zero recourse
- **No insurance:** You're liable for injuries, property damage, everything

### What to Do

**Before you even review the quote, verify:**
- General liability insurance (minimum $1M coverage)
- Workers' comp insurance (if they have employees)
- Contractor license (check your state's database)

**Ask for:** Certificate of insurance (COI) with YOUR address listed as additional insured.

If they can't provide it immediately, walk. Legitimate contractors have these documents ready to go because they know you'll ask.

## The Walk-Away Checklist

Run through this before you sign:

- ❌ Refuses to itemize costs
- ❌ Pressures you to sign today
- ❌ Materials marked up 40%+
- ❌ No clear timeline or start date
- ❌ No proof of license/insurance
- ❌ Won't provide references
- ❌ Asks for 50%+ upfront (30% is standard)
- ❌ No written contract or warranty terms

**If you checked 2+, walk away.**

## When Your Gut Says "Something's Off"

Sometimes there's no specific red flag. The contractor just feels... sketchy. Trust that.

Your subconscious is processing body language, inconsistencies, and subtle cues you might not consciously notice. If something feels wrong, it probably is.
