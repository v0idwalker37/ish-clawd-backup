# Kitchen Remodel Cost Breakdown: Real Numbers for Cabinets, Countertops & Everything Else

You want a new kitchen. You've gotten quotes ranging from $25,000 to $75,000 for what seems like the same work. How is that possible?

Kitchen remodels are notorious for price opacity. There are dozens of line items, multiple materials to choose from, and enough complexity that contractors can bury inflated costs where most homeowners will never find them.

This guide breaks down exactly what each component of a kitchen remodel should cost—from cabinets to countertops to the labor to install them. You'll know what's fair, what's padded, and where to push back.

## Kitchen Remodel Cost Overview by Size

Before diving into itemized costs, here are realistic total budgets by kitchen size:

| **Kitchen Size** | **Budget Remodel** | **Mid-Range Remodel** | **High-End Remodel** |
|------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|----------------------|
| **Small (70-100 sq ft)** | $15,000-$25,000 | $25,000-$45,000 | $45,000-$75,000+ |
| **Medium (100-150 sq ft)** | $25,000-$40,000 | $40,000-$65,000 | $65,000-$100,000+ |
| **Large (150-200+ sq ft)** | $40,000-$60,000 | $60,000-$90,000 | $90,000-$150,000+ |

**What defines each tier:**

**Budget:** Stock cabinets, laminate countertops, basic appliances, vinyl flooring, minimal structural changes

**Mid-Range:** Semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite countertops, quality appliances, tile or engineered hardwood flooring, some layout changes

**High-End:** Custom cabinets, premium countertops (marble, quartzite), high-end appliances, hardwood or luxury tile flooring, significant layout changes, designer lighting

## The Itemized Breakdown: Where Your Money Goes

### 1. Cabinets (30-40% of total budget)

Cabinets are the single largest expense in most kitchen remodels. The price variation is enormous.

#### Stock Cabinets (Budget)
**Cost:** $75-$200 per linear foot
- **Total for 20 linear feet:** $1,500-$4,000
- Pre-manufactured, standard sizes
- Limited styles and finishes
- Particle board or MDF construction
- Basic hardware included

**Brands:** IKEA, Hampton Bay (Home Depot), Continental Cabinets

#### Semi-Custom Cabinets (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $150-$400 per linear foot
- **Total for 20 linear feet:** $3,000-$8,000
- More size and finish options
- Better construction (plywood boxes)
- Soft-close hinges and drawer glides
- Dovetail drawer construction

**Brands:** KraftMaid, Merillat, American Woodmark, Thomasville

#### Custom Cabinets (High-End)
**Cost:** $500-$1,500+ per linear foot
- **Total for 20 linear feet:** $10,000-$30,000+
- Any size, style, finish
- Premium materials (solid wood, exotic veneers)
- Hand-crafted details
- Full customization

**Brands:** Local cabinet makers, Wood-Mode, Rutt, SieMatic

#### Installation Labor
**Cost:** $50-$100 per linear foot
- **Total for 20 linear feet:** $1,000-$2,000
- Includes removal of old cabinets
- Installation, leveling, securing
- Hardware installation

**Red flag:** If installation labor exceeds $125 per linear foot for standard cabinets, question it.

---

### 2. Countertops (10-15% of total budget)

Countertop pricing varies wildly by material, thickness, edge detail, and square footage.

#### Laminate (Budget)
**Cost:** $10-$40 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $500-$2,000
- Affordable, many colors
- Not heat-resistant
- Can chip or peel over time

#### Tile (Budget-Mid)
**Cost:** $15-$50 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $750-$2,500
- Durable, heat-resistant
- Grout lines require maintenance
- Can be DIY-friendly

#### Quartz (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $50-$120 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $2,500-$6,000
- Non-porous, low maintenance
- Consistent color and pattern
- Not heat-proof (hot pans can damage)

**Brands:** Caesarstone, Cambria, Silestone

#### Granite (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $40-$100 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $2,000-$5,000
- Natural stone, unique patterns
- Requires periodic sealing
- Heat-resistant

#### Marble (High-End)
**Cost:** $75-$250 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $3,750-$12,500
- Luxurious, classic appearance
- Stains and scratches easily
- High maintenance

#### Quartzite (High-End)
**Cost:** $70-$200 per square foot installed
- **50 sq ft kitchen:** $3,500-$10,000
- Natural stone, hard as granite
- Marble-like appearance
- Requires sealing

#### What's Included in "Installed" Price
- Template and measurement
- Fabrication (cutting, edge detail)
- Delivery and installation
- Seam sealing (if applicable)
- Sink cutout (first one)

**Red flags:**
- Charging separately for "template fee" ($200-$400) when quote says "installed"
- Fabrication charges that double the material cost
- Seam charges over $100 per seam
- Edge detail upcharges over $20 per linear foot for standard edges

### 3. Flooring (5-10% of total budget)

Kitchen flooring must handle traffic, spills, and temperature changes.

#### Vinyl/LVP (Budget)
**Cost:** $2-$7 per square foot installed
- **150 sq ft kitchen:** $300-$1,050
- Waterproof, durable
- Easy installation (can be DIY)
- Good for resale value

#### Ceramic/Porcelain Tile (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $5-$15 per square foot installed
- **150 sq ft kitchen:** $750-$2,250
- Highly durable, waterproof
- Cold underfoot (can add radiant heat)
- Professional installation recommended

#### Engineered Hardwood (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $6-$16 per square foot installed
- **150 sq ft kitchen:** $900-$2,400
- Real wood look, more stable than solid
- Not fully waterproof
- Refinishable (limited times)

#### Solid Hardwood (High-End)
**Cost:** $8-$20 per square foot installed
- **150 sq ft kitchen:** $1,200-$3,000
- Classic, timeless
- Not recommended for kitchens (water damage risk)
- Refinishable multiple times

**Installation labor typically:** $2-$5 per sq ft depending on complexity

**Red flag:** Charging $10+ per square foot for vinyl installation. Standard LVP installation is $2-$3 per sq ft.

### 4. Backsplash (2-5% of total budget)

Backsplash is where style meets function—and where costs can escalate quickly.

#### Ceramic Tile (Budget)
**Cost:** $7-$15 per square foot installed
- **40 sq ft backsplash:** $280-$600
- Standard subway tile or simple patterns
- Durable, easy to clean

#### Glass Tile (Mid-Range)
**Cost:** $15-$40 per square foot installed
- **40 sq ft backsplash:** $600-$1,600
- Reflective, modern look
- More difficult to install (higher labor)

#### Natural Stone/Marble (High-End)
**Cost:** $20-$60 per square foot installed
- **40 sq ft backsplash:** $800-$2,400
- Luxurious appearance
- Requires sealing
- Can stain from cooking splatters

#### Designer/Custom Tile (High-End)
**Cost:** $30-$100+ per square foot installed
- **40 sq ft backsplash:** $1,200-$4,000+
- Handmade, artisan tiles
- Unique patterns or mosaics
- Labor-intensive installation

**Installation labor:** $5-$15 per sq ft depending on tile type and pattern complexity

**Red flag:** Simple subway tile installation labor over $8 per sq ft. Standard is $4-$6 per sq ft.

### 5. Plumbing (5-10% of total budget)

Plumbing costs vary dramatically based on whether you're relocating fixtures or simply replacing them.

#### Sink Replacement (No Relocation)
**Cost:** $200-$600
- Disconnect old sink
- Install new sink (undermount or drop-in)
- Connect supply lines and drain
- 2-4 hours of labor

#### Faucet Installation
**Cost:** $150-$350
- Remove old faucet
- Install new faucet
- Connect supply lines
- Test for leaks
- 1-2 hours of labor

#### Garbage Disposal Installation
**Cost:** $150-$400
- **Unit cost:** $75-$250
- **Labor:** $75-$150
- Electrical connection required
- 1-2 hours

#### Dishwasher Installation
**Cost:** $150-$400 (labor only; appliance separate)
- Connect water supply
- Connect drain line
- Level and secure unit
- Test operation
- 2-3 hours

#### Relocating Plumbing (Major Cost)
**Cost:** $1,500-$5,000+ depending on distance
- Rerouting water supply lines
- Relocating drain pipes
- May require opening walls/floors
- Requires permits and inspections

**Typical plumber hourly rate:** $75-$150 per hour (varies by region)

**Red flag:** Charging $800 to install a sink when no relocation is involved. Standard installation is $200-$400.

### 6. Electrical (5-10% of total budget)

Kitchens require significant electrical work: outlets, lighting, appliance circuits.

#### Outlet Installation (Standard)
**Cost:** $100-$200 per outlet
- Includes box, outlet, wiring (short run)
- GFCI outlets (required in kitchens)
- 1-2 hours per outlet

#### Under-Cabinet Lighting
**Cost:** $250-$800 total
- LED strip lighting: $30-$60 per linear foot installed
- Puck lights: $50-$100 per light installed
- Hardwired vs plug-in affects cost

#### Recessed Lighting
**Cost:** $150-$300 per light installed
- Includes fixture, housing, trim
- Wiring to switch
- Cutting ceiling holes
- 2-3 hours per light

#### Pendant/Chandelier Installation
**Cost:** $100-$300 per fixture (labor only)
- Mounting electrical box
- Wiring to switch
- Hanging and securing fixture
- 1-2 hours per fixture

#### Appliance Circuits (New)
**Cost:** $300-$800 per circuit
- 240V for range/oven: $400-$800
- 120V for dishwasher/disposal: $300-$500
- Running wire from panel to location
- Dedicated circuit required by code

**Typical electrician hourly rate:** $75-$150 per hour (varies by region)

**Red flag:** Charging $500 to install a single standard outlet. Unless significant rewiring is needed, this is padding.

### 7. Labor (20-35% of total budget)

Beyond specialized trades (plumber, electrician), general contractor labor includes:

#### Demolition
**Cost:** $1,000-$3,000 for average kitchen

- Disconnect appliances
- Haul away debris
- Dumpster rental: $400-$800

**Time:** 1-3 days depending on scope

#### Drywall Repair/Painting
**Cost:** $1,500-$4,000
- Patching walls after cabinet removal
- Skim coating/texturing
- Priming and painting walls/ceiling
- 2 coats of paint

**Typical painting cost:** $2-$6 per sq ft

#### General Carpentry
**Cost:** $50-$100 per hour
- Trimwork and molding
- Building custom shelving
- Installing hardware
- Miscellaneous adjustments

**Red flag:** "Labor" line items without specifics. Insist on breakdown by task and estimated hours.

## Where Contractors Pad Quotes the Most

Based on analysis of thousands of quotes, here's where padding is most common:

### 1. **Labor Hours**
**The scam:** Quoting 80 hours for cabinet installation that should take 20.

**How to catch it:** Ask for task-specific labor estimates and compare to industry standards.

### 2. **Material Markups**
**The scam:** Charging $8,000 for cabinets they purchased for $3,000.

**How to catch it:** Research the specific products they're quoting and verify retail prices. Reasonable markup is 20-40%.

### 3. **"Project Management" Fees**
**The scam:** Adding 15-20% "project management" on top of general contractor fees.

**How to catch it:** GC overhead should be built into their rates, not an extra line item. If it's separate, it should be 5-10% max and clearly defined.

### 4. **Vague Contingency**
**The scam:** 20% contingency that's never itemized or returned if unused.

**How to catch it:** Contingency should be 10-15% max and only charged if issues arise (with documentation).

### 5. **Inflated Demo Costs**
**The scam:** $4,000 for demolition that takes one day with a 2-person crew.

**How to catch it:** Demo typically costs $1,000-$2,500 for average kitchen. If it's higher, ask why.

### 6. **Permit Fees**
**The scam:** Charging $1,200 for permits that actually cost $300.

**How to catch it:** Call your local building department and ask what permits are required and their cost.

## Money-Saving Tips That Don't Sacrifice Quality

You can reduce costs without compromising the integrity of your remodel:

### 1. **Keep the Layout**
Moving plumbing and electrical is expensive ($3,000-$10,000+). If you can work with your existing layout, you'll save significantly.

### 2. **Reface Instead of Replace Cabinets**
If your cabinet boxes are solid, refacing (new doors, drawer fronts, veneering the frames) costs 40-50% less than full replacement.

**Cost:** $4,000-$9,000 vs. $8,000-$20,000 for new cabinets

### 3. **Choose Stock or Semi-Custom Cabinets**
Custom cabinets cost 2-3x more than semi-custom with minimal functional difference for most homeowners.

### 4. **Buy Appliances Yourself**
Contractors often mark up appliances 20-30%. Buy directly during holiday sales (Memorial Day, Black Friday, Labor Day) and save $1,000-$3,000.

### 5. **Do Your Own Demo**
If you're handy, demolition is labor-intensive but not technically difficult. Doing it yourself saves $1,000-$2,500.

**What you'll need:**
- Dumpster rental ($400-$800)
- Basic tools (pry bars, hammer, utility knife)
- Safety gear (gloves, goggles, dust mask)
- 1-3 days of hard work

### 6. **Choose Quartz Over Marble**
Quartz looks nearly identical to marble but costs 30-50% less and requires no maintenance.

### 7. **Limit Tile Patterns**
Simple tile layouts (subway, herringbone) cost less in labor than intricate mosaics or custom patterns.

### 8. **Paint Cabinets Instead of Replacing**
Professional cabinet painting costs $3,000-$8,000 vs. $10,000-$30,000 for new cabinets. If your cabinets are structurally sound, this is a huge savings.

### 9. **Use Open Shelving Strategically**
Open shelves cost significantly less than upper cabinets. Use them for 20-30% of upper storage to save $1,000-$3,000.

### 10. **Get Multiple Quotes**
This is obvious but critical. Three quotes from licensed, insured contractors will show you the market range and highlight outliers.

## Timeline Expectations: How Long Should It Take?

Understanding timeline helps you spot contractors who are padding hours or dragging out projects.

### Small Kitchen (70-100 sq ft)
**Timeline:** 3-5 weeks

- Week 1: Demo, rough plumbing/electrical
- Week 2: Drywall repair, cabinet installation
- Week 3: Countertops templated and installed
- Week 4: Flooring, backsplash, painting
- Week 5: Final trim, appliances, punch list

### Medium Kitchen (100-150 sq ft)
**Timeline:** 4-7 weeks

- Week 1: Demo and rough work
- Week 2-3: Plumbing/electrical, cabinets
- Week 4: Countertops
- Week 5: Flooring and backsplash
- Week 6: Painting and trim
- Week 7: Appliances, fixtures, final touches

### Large Kitchen (150-200+ sq ft)
**Timeline:** 6-10 weeks

- Week 1-2: Demo, structural work, rough plumbing/electrical
- Week 3-4: Cabinet installation
- Week 5: Countertop template and installation
- Week 6-7: Flooring, backsplash
- Week 8: Painting, trim work
- Week 9: Appliance installation, final electrical/plumbing
- Week 10: Punch list and final inspection

**Red flag:** If a contractor says a small kitchen will take 12 weeks, they're either overbooked (working on multiple jobs simultaneously) or padding the timeline.
